================================================================================
ANNOTATION GUIDE — DOMESTIC PROCEEDINGS IN ECtHR ARTICLE 6 JUDGMENTS
Grounded in ontology/echr.ttl, version 3.7.0.
Guide version 1.4 — §3.2 gains `part_of`/`occurs_during`; §5.1 gains
`is_pending`, `non_occurring` and `matter` (§6.7, new); §5.6 (new) gains
`subject_matter`. Ontology bumped to 3.7.0 for the `matter`/`hasMatter`
vocabulary.
================================================================================

Read this once before annotating, and keep §4 (which events to record) and §6
(controlled vocabularies) open while you work.

================================================================================
1. WHAT YOU ARE PRODUCING
================================================================================

For each document: the list of domestic events the judgment describes, one YAML
record each, in the schema at annotation/annotation_schema.yaml.

Read the whole judgment first, then annotate. Order your records by decision
date, earliest first.

================================================================================
2. SCOPE — WHICH PART OF THE JUDGMENT
================================================================================

IN SCOPE: the domestic (national) procedural history, wherever the judgment
narrates it — normally "THE FACTS" / "THE CIRCUMSTANCES OF THE CASE", and in
decisions also "PROCEDURE".

OUT OF SCOPE, always:
  - Proceedings before the European Court of Human Rights itself. The Strasbourg
    application, its communication, the Chamber and Grand Chamber. The domestic
    chain ends at the last national decision.
  - "RELEVANT DOMESTIC LAW AND PRACTICE" — statutes, and decisions of other
    courts cited as authority rather than as steps in this applicant's case.
  - Another person's case cited as legal authority or for comparison. NOT the
    same as proceedings involving other people that form part of this
    applicant's own history — those are in scope; see §4.3.
  - The Court's own legal reasoning, admissibility analysis, and Article 41
    just-satisfaction award.

================================================================================
3. THE UNIT — ONE EVENT OR TWO?
================================================================================

The ontology's definition (echr:DomesticProceeding):

    "One set of proceedings before a single national court or authority in the
     domestic chain (e.g. first-instance trial, appeal, cassation)."

So the unit is ONE AUTHORITY DECIDING ONCE. Consequences, all of which follow
from that sentence and are not additional rules:

  - A hearing and the judgment closing it on the same date are ONE event, not
    two. The hearing is a step inside the proceeding, not a proceeding.
  - Several hearings across two years before one court, ending in one judgment,
    are ONE event. Put the institution date in start_date and the judgment date
    in decision_date.
  - A first-instance trial and its appeal are TWO events, linked by `follows`.
  - Remittal makes a new event even before the same court. The ontology says so
    explicitly (scopeNote on echr:DomesticProceeding): "Where a case is remitted
    and retried before the same court, the retrial is a separate proceeding
    linked by echr:followsProceeding."
  - An administrative decision and the court challenge to it are TWO events —
    scopeNote on echr:AdministrativeAction — the challenge linked back with
    `follows`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 A FILING AND ITS DECISION ARE TWO EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where the text separately narrates the institution of a proceeding and the
decision closing it, record TWO events:

    THE FILING     start_date, NO decision_date, outcome Unknown,
                   outcome_direction Unknown, quote = the filing sentence.
                   `follows` = whatever the filing responded to (null if it
                   heads a chain).

    THE DECISION   decision_date, NO start_date, the real outcome and
                   outcome_direction, quote = the decision sentence.
                   `part_of` = the filing event. NO `follows` (see §3.2).

Both carry the same type, body, authority_kind, level and proceeding_type —
they are two acts of ONE proceeding, and §3.2 is how you say so. Anything that
followed that proceeding — an appeal, an enforcement step — follows THE FILING,
which is the id that stands for the proceeding as a whole. Where the chain is
terminal, final_domestic_decision goes on THE DECISION, because that is the act
that decided it.

APPLY THIS WHENEVER THE TEXT NARRATES BOTH ACTS, however short the interval and
whether or not anything else happened in between. Do not weigh how long the
proceeding took or how much fell in the gap; the question is only whether the
judgment gives you two sentences or one.

  - "On 15 March 1999 the applicant instituted civil proceedings ... On 5 June
    2003 the court dismissed the claim." — TWO events, even though nothing else
    is narrated in the four years between.
  - "On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed a claim brought in 1999." — ONE event.
    The institution is reported, not narrated: there is no second act to quote.
    Put 1999 in start_date and 2003 in decision_date.
  - A request and the decision refusing it, both narrated, are TWO events. Three
    refused requests, each narrated twice, are six.

AN UNDATED FILING DOES NOT GET ITS OWN EVENT. "The prosecutor appealed", with no
date, is narrated but undatable: a separate record for it would carry no
start_date and no decision_date, and every event must be placed in time. Keep it
inside the decision's record and say so in `note`. Split only where you can put
a date in start_date.

THE REASON IS THE QUOTE. `quote` must be the passage that evidences THIS event
(§5). A folded record has two distinct textual acts and can only carry one of
them, so one of its two dates is always left unsupported — and it is usually the
decision, since annotators reach for the sentence that names the court. Splitting
is what makes every event's quote actually support that event.

The folding rules above are unaffected. Several hearings ending in one judgment
are still ONE event: a hearing is a step inside a proceeding, not a separate act
of instituting or deciding.

WHEN THE TEXT IS AMBIGUOUS about whether something is one event or two, record
one event and write why in `note`. Do not split on suspicion. §3.1 is not a
matter of suspicion: either the text narrates the filing as its own act, with a
sentence you can quote, or it does not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 `part_of` — TWO ACTS, ONE PROCEEDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Splitting under §3.1 gives you two RECORDS, but they are still one PROCEEDING:
one authority, one procedure, decided once. `part_of` says which proceeding a
record is an act of.

    - id: 3                          the FILING. No `part_of`, so it heads its
      start_date: "1999-03-29"       own proceeding and its id is the name of
      follows: 1                     that proceeding for the rest of the file.

    - id: 7                          the DECISION. `part_of: 3` makes it a
      part_of: 3                     second act of the SAME proceeding.
      decision_date: "2003-09-02"    No `follows` — see below.

THE ID OF THE FIRST ACT IS THE NAME OF THE PROCEEDING. A record with no
`part_of` is a proceeding in its own right; every other record names the one it
belongs to. Grouping is ONE LEVEL DEEP: the target of `part_of` must not itself
carry `part_of`.

AN ACT WITH `part_of` HAS NO `follows`. `follows` records a procedural step
from one proceeding to the next (§5.4). Two acts of the same proceeding are not
a step between proceedings — they are ordered by their own dates, and nothing
more needs saying. Writing `follows` there asserts a step that did not happen.

ALL ACTS OF ONE PROCEEDING MUST AGREE on type, body, authority_kind, level and
proceeding_type. Those five describe the proceeding, not the act, so if two acts
disagree, one of them is wrong or they are not the same proceeding after all.
check_annotations.py enforces this, and it is the main thing `part_of` buys.

WHAT GOES IN A GROUP, AND WHAT DOES NOT:

  YES  A §3.1 filing and its decision.
  YES  Two rulings by the same judge in the same track — two interim awards by
       the same urgent-applications judge, say.
  NO   The same court deciding a LATER, separate proceeding. Remittal, retrial
       and a fresh application all make a new proceeding (§3) even before the
       same court: those are `follows`, not `part_of`.
  NO   A different authority. If the body differs in substance, it is not the
       same proceeding — see §5.5. A body that differs only in wording ("the
       Łask District Court" vs "the Łask District Court ( Sąd Rejonowy)") IS
       the same authority; write it the same way in both acts.

WHERE THE PROCEEDING'S SPAN AND OUTCOME LIVE. Nowhere: they are read off its
acts. The span runs from the earliest to the latest date recorded, and the
disposition is that of the last act. Both are approximations — the acts you have
are the ones the text dated — which is why they are derived rather than written
down as if they were asserted by the judgment.

================================================================================
4. WHICH EVENTS TO RECORD
================================================================================

IS IT ONE OF THE FOUR echr:DomesticEvent SUBCLASSES?

If you can type it, annotate it. If it is none of the four, it is not an event.
That is the whole rule, and it is deliberately generous: echr:DomesticEvent is
defined as "ANY milestone in the domestic procedural history narrated in the
facts".

RECORD ALL LEGALLY RELEVANT EVENTS. When a thing could plausibly be typed and it
mattered legally -- it moved the case, created or removed a right, started or
stopped a clock, authorised or ended a deprivation of liberty -- record it. An
arrest, a search ordered by an investigator, a bill of indictment, a detention
order, the lifting of an immunity, an order that was never executed: all of these
are events. The default is to RECORD, and §4.4 is for saying you were unsure, not
for leaving things out.

The reason is asymmetry. An event you recorded and should not have is visible and
can be removed; an event you never recorded is invisible and cannot be recovered
by anything downstream.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 The four types
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Definitions verbatim from ontology/echr.ttl. Ask them in this order.

  echr:DomesticProceeding
      "One set of proceedings before a single national court or authority in the
       domestic chain (e.g. first-instance trial, appeal, cassation)."
      The default for anything before a court or tribunal.

  echr:AdministrativeAction
      "A decision by a state or municipal body outside a formal trial (e.g.
       appointing a guardian, denying a welfare request, refusing a permit)."

  echr:EnforcementAction
      "The physical or bureaucratic execution of an earlier decision (e.g.
       forced relocation to an institution, seizure of property, removal from
       the country)."

  echr:ProsecutorialReview
      "An internal decision or appeal within a public prosecutor's office (e.g.
       a regional prosecutor's refusal to act, reviewed by the prosecutor's
       office above it), distinct from a proceeding before a court."
      Use DomesticProceeding instead once the matter reaches a court.

`type` IS NOT THE SAME QUESTION AS `authority_kind` (§5.2, §6.6), and they can
come apart. `authority_kind` describes the deciding body; `type` describes
what kind of step this was. They usually align -- a Prosecutorial authority
acting of its own motion, with no court involved, is `type: ProsecutorialReview`
-- but not always: a court reviewing a prosecutor's decision is
`type: DomesticProceeding` even though a prosecutorial act sits inside it, and
an administrative body's decision being physically enforced is
`type: EnforcementAction` regardless of who the enforcing authority is. Read
what was DONE at this event, not just who did it.

A PROCEEDING STILL RUNNING IS STILL AN EVENT. Nothing above requires a decision
to have been reached. A claim lodged and never determined is a DomesticProceeding
with a start_date, no decision_date and outcome Unknown. The ontology carries
echr:isPending for exactly this situation.

An event does not need the applicant to be a party, and does not need to belong
to the proceedings actually complained of. See 4.3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 The two exclusions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Only two things are ruled out, and neither is about who acted:

  NOT DOMESTIC.  Proceedings before the European Court of Human Rights itself.
  The domestic history ends at the last national decision.

  NOT THIS HISTORY.  Another case cited as legal authority or for comparison —
  "as the Court held in X v. Y", a leading domestic judgment quoted for the rule
  it lays down. That is law, not history.

Beyond those two, if it types, it counts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Scope is the whole narrated history, not the exhaustion chain
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All of these are in, and none is a special case:

  - PRIOR proceedings — the earlier conviction, the administrative decision
    later challenged, the guardianship order that preceded everything. The
    scopeNote on echr:AdministrativeAction expects exactly this: the decision is
    its own event and the court challenge follows it.
  - PARALLEL proceedings — a civil claim running alongside the criminal case.
    Independent chains are normal; a document routinely has several, each with
    its own `follows: null` head.
  - CONSEQUENT proceedings — enforcement, reopening, supervisory review, after
    the chain had otherwise closed.
  - Proceedings where THE APPLICANT WAS NOT A PARTY but which the facts narrate
    as part of their history: a guardian's application concerning the applicant,
    a prosecutor's decision about a relative's death. echr:Party is "a person,
    company or public body taking part in the domestic proceedings, INCLUDING
    the future applicant" — the applicant is one possible party, not a
    requirement.

Which events exhausted domestic remedies is recorded by
`final_domestic_decision` (§5), so that distinction is carried by a field rather
than by leaving events out.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 When you cannot decide
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Some things sit genuinely on the boundary. A crime-scene inspection, an arrest,
a registration made at someone's request, an agreement signed between private
parties: whether these are milestones in the procedural history or merely things
that happened is a real question, and the ontology does not settle it.

Do not agonise, and do not invent a rule. RECORD THE EVENT -- always record it --
AND MARK IT:

    borderline: true
    borderline_reason: "investigative step, no decision by any body"

Then carry on. The flag costs you a few seconds and makes the call visible
instead of silent, so a reader can see exactly which events you were unsure
about and why.

Mark borderline only where you would expect a careful colleague to disagree with
you — not merely where you had to think about it. If you find yourself flagging
most of a document, the boundary is not the problem and something else has gone
wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5 One practice note
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Annotate what the judgment states, not what must have happened. An event you
infer from the shape of the procedure ("the appeal must have been registered")
is not in the document and is not annotated. This is a rule about evidence, not
about scope: everything in §4 assumes the text says so.

================================================================================
5. THE FIELDS, AND THE TRIPLES THEY STAND FOR
================================================================================

Every YAML key maps to exactly one ontology construct, listed beside it. A field
means what the ontology says it means and nothing more.

Annotate every field on every event.

Throughout: E is the event node, A the deciding authority, P a party, p a
participation node, D the case document.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 The event
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  id
      Your local number, 1..n, ordered by decision_date, earliest first — or by
      start_date for a filing event, which has no decision_date (§3.1).
      Not a triple — `follows` uses it, and so does the alignment pass.
      Implied: D echr:hasDomesticProceeding E .

  type
      E a echr:<Class> .
      One of the four in §4.1: DomesticProceeding, AdministrativeAction,
      EnforcementAction, ProsecutorialReview.

  start_date
      E echr:hasProceedingStartDate "<date>" .
      When the event was instituted. Omit if the text does not give it, and on
      the decision half of a pair split under §3.1.

  decision_date
      E echr:hasDecisionDate "<date>" .
      Date of the decision that closed the event. Omit if still running, and on
      the filing half of a pair split under §3.1.

      A record carrying BOTH dates is now the exception, not the norm. It means
      the institution was reported rather than narrated — no quotable sentence of
      its own — so there was only ever one act to record. See §3.1.

      Both dates are echr:PartialDate: a full date, a year and month, or a bare
      year — as precise as the text allows and no more. "in the spring of 2003"
      is "2003". ALWAYS QUOTE DATES in the YAML: "2004-03-14", "2004-03",
      "2004". Unquoted, YAML reads those three as a date, a string and an
      integer.

  level
      E echr:hasInstanceLevel echr:Level<X> .   Closed vocabulary, §6.1.

  proceeding_type
      E echr:hasProceedingType echr:Type<X> .   Closed vocabulary, §6.2.

  outcome
      E echr:hasOutcome echr:Outcome<X> .       Closed vocabulary, §6.3.
      Unknown where the event is still running.

  outcome_direction
      E echr:hasOutcomeDirection echr:Direction<X> .  Closed vocabulary, §6.4.
      "The applicant" means the future Strasbourg applicant, even where they
      were not yet an applicant at the time.

  final_domestic_decision
      E echr:isFinalDomesticDecision true .
      Mark the event that concluded EACH chain -- the last decision the text
      actually records in it, full stop. Write `true` on that event and omit
      the field everywhere else — this is the one field where omission means
      false rather than "the text does not say".

      Rules, in order:
        - RESPONDENT STATE ONLY. Domestic remedies within the meaning of
          Article 35 § 1 are remedies in the respondent State's own legal
          order. Never write `true` on an event whose `jurisdiction_state`
          (§5.2) is not the respondent State, however terminal that event is
          within its own narrative — a foreign court's final word (a Michigan
          divorce court, an Uzbek conviction) is not domestic-remedy
          exhaustion for a Strasbourg application against Turkey or Austria.
        - ONE PER CHAIN, not one per document. A judgment with two independent
          chains has two flags. §3 defines the chain.
        - NOT NECESSARILY THE LATEST EVENT. Enforcement, or an unrelated later
          proceeding, can be dated after the decision that actually exhausted
          the remedy. Follow `follows` to the end of the chain; do not sort by
          date.
        - NOT GATED ON THE APPLICATION DATE. Mark the chain's own terminal
          decision even where the text dates it after the ECHR application was
          lodged. Whether that decision served exhaustion for the application
          actually filed is a downstream inference — compare this event's date
          to the application date — not something this field decides by
          omission; building that judgement into whether the flag gets
          asserted at all throws the fact away rather than just leaving the
          inference for later. A chain the text leaves genuinely open (no
          concluding decision narrated at all) still gets no flag, because it
          has no terminal event to mark — that is a different case from one
          whose terminal event merely falls late.
        - Where the applicant stopped short of the highest available instance,
          flag the last decision they actually obtained and say so in `note`.
          You are recording what happened, not ruling on whether remedies were
          properly exhausted — that is the Court's job, not an annotation task.

  is_pending
      E echr:isPending true .
      true where the text reports THIS proceeding as still undecided at the
      time of the ECHR application — "the appeal is currently pending", "has
      yet to rule". Omit otherwise; omission does not mean false, it means the
      text does not flag it either way.

      A proceeding recorded this way is `start_date`, no `decision_date`,
      `outcome: Unknown` — exactly what a §3.1 filing looks like. `is_pending`
      is what tells the two apart: a filing is Unknown because its decision
      hasn't been written yet (it exists, elsewhere in this file, at the id
      named by `part_of`); a pending proceeding is Unknown because the text
      says there is no decision to write. Set it on the head of the proceeding
      only, never on one of its acts (§3.2) — pendency is a property of the
      whole thing, not of any one act in it.

  non_occurring
      E echr:isNonOccurring true .
      true where the text narrates this event as something that was NOT
      done -- "the applicant did not appeal", "no request for release was
      ever lodged" -- rather than something that happened. Omit for every
      ordinary event; omission does not mean the event went smoothly, it
      means non-occurrence was never in question.

      Type the event as what it would have been had it happened (an appeal
      never lodged is still `type: DomesticProceeding`, `level: Appeal`),
      quote the words that state the negation, and give it no
      `decision_date` -- nothing was decided, because nothing happened.
      `outcome` and `outcome_direction` are Unknown for the same reason.

      Distinct from `is_pending`: a pending proceeding is real and under
      way, just not yet decided; a non-occurring one never started. Do not
      use `borderline` for this -- a narrated non-event is not a hard call
      about scope, it is an ordinary event carrying one extra flag.

  matter
      E echr:hasMatter echr:Matter<X> .   Closed vocabulary, §6.7.
      What THIS EVENT was substantively about -- bail, damages, arrest --
      as distinct from `proceeding_type` (the procedural TRACK: civil,
      criminal, administrative) and from `type` (the procedural SHAPE: a
      proceeding, an administrative action). Bail and detention are both
      `type: DomesticProceeding`, both `proceeding_type: Criminal`, and
      substantively nothing alike; `matter` is what tells them apart.

      UNLIKE EVERY OTHER CLOSED VOCABULARY IN THIS GUIDE, `matter` is NOT
      filled on every event -- §7's "always write Unknown" rule does not
      apply here. Omit it where the event is not substantively about any
      of §6.7's values: a remittal, a transfer of jurisdiction, a
      procedural ruling with no substantive content of its own. Write
      `Other` only where the event IS about one identifiable substantive
      matter that the vocabulary does not name; write `Unknown` only
      where it is clearly about one of these matters but the text does
      not let you tell which. Neither is a catch-all for "no substantive
      matter" -- that case is an omission, not a value.

      NOT FUNCTIONAL: give a list, e.g. `matter: [Detention, Bail]`, where
      one decision genuinely rules on more than one (an order continuing
      detention while also setting bail conditions).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 The deciding authority
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  body
      E echr:hasCourt A .   A echr:hasAuthorityName "<body>" .
      The authority's name VERBATIM as this passage writes it. If the text says
      "the Regional Court", write that, not the fuller name from elsewhere in
      the judgment. Two spellings of one authority are still one authority —
      §8.

  authority_kind
      A echr:hasAuthorityKind echr:Authority<X> .  Closed vocabulary, §6.6.

  jurisdiction_state
      A echr:hasJurisdictionState "<state>" .
      The State whose legal order this authority belongs to. Almost always
      the respondent State (§4.3 already puts foreign proceedings in scope
      where the facts narrate them — this is what tells the two apart on the
      record itself, not just in a note).

      Write the plain English name of the state ("Turkey", "the United
      States", "Uzbekistan"), not an ISO code and not the demonym used in the
      passage ("Turkish police" -> "Turkey"). Not a closed vocabulary — same
      treatment as echr:hasNationality (§5.3): free text, because the corpus
      includes contested and historical states this cannot be normalised
      against a fixed list without loss.

      A THIRD-STATE AUTHORITY (a foreign court, prosecutor or government named
      in the facts) still gets this field — that is the whole point of having
      it: it is what makes the authority's state a fact on the record instead
      of something a reader has to infer from `body` or a borderline note.
      Mark such an event `borderline` per §4.4 as before; `jurisdiction_state`
      does not replace that flag, it makes the flag checkable.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 The parties
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  parties
      A list. For each entry, with name N, side S:
          E echr:hasParticipation p .
          p echr:participatingParty P .
          p echr:hasPartySide echr:Side<S> .
          P echr:hasPartyName "<N>"   (and P echr:hasPersonName "<N>" if human)

      ONE ENTRY PER PARTY PER EVENT. A party appearing at three instances
      appears in three events' party lists, once each.

      name
          As the text names them: "the applicant", "M.N.", "Scholz AG".

      side
          Closed vocabulary, §6.8. Side is per EVENT, not per person: a
          defendant at trial who appeals is Responding in the trial event and
          Initiating in the appeal event. That is the whole reason participation
          is a node rather than two columns.

      natural_person
          true for one human being; false for a company, a public body, or a
          group the text never separates.
          true  -> P a echr:NaturalPerson .
          The ontology is strict here: a node standing for several people ("the
          applicants", "M.N. and Others") is NOT a natural person. Where the
          text names them individually, make one entry each; where it never
          separates them, make one entry with natural_person: false.

      THE DECIDING BODY IS NEVER A PARTY TO THE EVENT IT DECIDED. A body can
      of course be a party to a DIFFERENT event in the chain — a prosecutor who
      decided at first instance may be the responding party on appeal.

      A lawyer acting for a party is representation, not participation. Record
      a lawyer only where the lawyer was themselves a party.

      THE FILER OR MOVANT IS A PARTY, side Initiating, whoever it is --
      including a prosecutor bringing a case of their own motion or an
      administrative body applying for a measure. Do not let the deciding
      body of THIS event crowd out who moved it: they are different roles,
      and a public body can hold both across different events in the chain
      (§5.5).

      An empty list `[]` says the text names no party, and is worth writing —
      it records that you looked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 The chain
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  follows
      E echr:followsProceeding E' .
      The `id` of the event immediately before this one, or null.

      "Immediately before" is PROCEDURAL, not chronological: an appeal follows
      the decision it appeals, an enforcement follows what it enforces, a court
      challenge follows the administrative decision challenged. Two events
      merely consecutive in time, with no procedural relation, are not linked —
      follows is null.

      Parallel tracks are normal. Several events may share one predecessor, and
      a document may hold several unconnected chains, each with its own null
      head. Record only a link the text supports.

      echr:followsProceeding is NOT owl:FunctionalProperty: give a LIST, e.g.
      `follows: [3, 6]`, where a single decision disposes of more than one
      predecessor (worked example 7, annotation_schema.yaml) — for instance one
      ruling that grants one party's appeal against a transfer of jurisdiction
      AND, on another party's appeal, quashes a separate enforcement order.
      Do not force such a decision onto a single id, and do not split it into
      two events merely to give each predecessor its own link — the decision is
      one event with two procedural antecedents; record it that way.

      BOTH ENDS MUST BE PROCEEDINGS. `follows` relates one proceeding to the
      next, so neither this event nor its target may carry `part_of` (§3.2).
      If the antecedent you want to name is an act inside a proceeding, name
      the proceeding — the id that carries no `part_of`.

  part_of
      A echr:partOfProceeding P .
      The `id` of the proceeding this record is one act of, or omit. See §3.2:
      omit it and this record IS a proceeding. Mutually exclusive with
      `follows` and with `occurs_during`.

  occurs_during
      P echr:occursDuring P' .
      The `id` of a proceeding within whose span this one ran, or omit.

      USE IT WHERE `follows` WOULD BE A LIE. `follows` says one proceeding came
      after another had been decided. Some proceedings instead begin AND end
      while another is still undecided:

        - an interim award made by an urgent-applications judge while the main
          claim it belongs to is years from judgment;
        - a reference to a higher court made out of a pending proceeding and
          answered back into it;
        - a bail application decided in the middle of a trial.

      In each the container had not been decided, so `follows` cannot apply;
      write `follows: null` and `occurs_during: <container>`.

      NOT THE SAME AS `part_of`. Ask who acted. If the SAME authority decided an
      interlocutory question inside its own proceeding, that is a second act of
      that proceeding: `part_of`. If a DIFFERENT authority or a distinct
      procedure ran alongside — a référé judge, a higher court answering a
      reference — it is its own proceeding, contained but not part of:
      `occurs_during`. Both ends must be proceedings, as with `follows`.

      DO NOT REACH FOR IT to record that two proceedings merely overlap in
      time. The container must be a proceeding the contained one ran inside of
      procedurally, and the text must support it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.5 Evidence and audit
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  quote
      E echr:hasSupportingQuote "<text>" .
      A short verbatim span from the judgment establishing this event. COPY AND
      PASTE IT. Do not retype, tidy punctuation, or stitch together text from
      two passages. One sentence is usually enough.

  borderline
      Not a triple. true where you could not confidently decide whether this
      belongs in the annotation at all — see §4.4. Omit otherwise.

  borderline_reason
      Not a triple. One line saying what made it a hard call. Required whenever
      borderline is true.

  note
      Not a triple -- annotator-only, kept out of the graph. Free text. Use it
      whenever you made a call someone could reasonably make differently — see
      §9. `borderline` is for whether the event belongs at all; `note` is for
      everything else.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6 Document-level fields
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These sit outside `proceedings`, once per file — see annotation_schema.yaml.

  subject_matter
      D echr:hasSubjectMatter "<text>" .
      Free text, open vocabulary (deliberately, unlike proceeding_type): what
      the underlying dispute was actually ABOUT, in your own words -- "custody
      of the applicant's children", "compensation for unlawful detention",
      "planning permission for an extension". Not functional: give more than
      one value where the document's several chains are about different
      things. There is no fixed list to pick from; write what the document is
      about and let the vocabulary emerge from the corpus.

  document_note
      Not a triple. Free text at the end of the file: an unusual structure, a
      section judged out of scope, a chain that could not be resolved. Read
      during disagreement analysis.

================================================================================
6. CONTROLLED VOCABULARIES — CLOSED, WRITE THE SHORT FORM
================================================================================
Write the short form given in CAPS below; the lifter expands it to the IRI.
Every vocabulary is closed. If nothing fits, use the vocabulary's own escape
value, never a word of your own.

6.1 level  (echr:InstanceLevel)
    Investigative           investigative or pre-trial
    AdministrativeReview    administrative or quasi-judicial review
    FirstInstance           first instance
    Appeal                  appeal
    Cassation               cassation or supreme court
    CaseReview              a review of an already-decided stage of the case by
                            a body external to the panel/instance that decided
                            it, outside the ordinary appeal ladder (e.g. a
                            presidium-level supervisory review, an
                            extraordinary review of a final decision) --
                            distinct from Reopening, which recommences the
                            whole proceeding rather than reviewing one stage
    Reopening               reopening or retrial
    Unknown                 escape value

6.2 proceeding_type  (echr:ProceedingType)
    Criminal  Civil  Administrative  Constitutional  Disciplinary
    Other  Unknown

6.3 outcome  (echr:ProceedingOutcome)
    MeritsDecided       a substantive decision was reached — conviction,
                        acquittal, claim granted or dismissed, measure imposed
    Inadmissible        dismissed on procedural grounds without reaching merits
    SettledOrWithdrawn  discontinued, settled out of court, or withdrawn
    Remitted            decision below overturned and sent back down
    Other               escape value
    Unknown             escape value — including where still pending

6.4 outcome_direction  (echr:OutcomeDirection)
    FavourableToApplicant   the substance of THIS decision came out the way
                            the applicant wanted, in full: the claim granted,
                            the charge not made out, the appeal that sought
                            this result succeeded.
    UnfavourableToApplicant the substance of THIS decision came out against
                            the applicant, in full: the claim dismissed, the
                            charge made out, the appeal that sought this
                            result failed.
    Mixed                   THIS SINGLE decision itself grants the applicant
                            something AND denies the applicant something
                            else, on the SAME claim, charge or request — not
                            two different proceedings that happened to
                            disagree. Typical shapes: a claim "upheld in
                            part"; a defendant convicted on one count and
                            acquitted on another; a sentence reduced but the
                            conviction upheld; damages awarded at less than
                            sought where the judgment itself frames it as a
                            partial award rather than merely quantifying an
                            already-granted claim.
                            NOT Mixed:
                              - a later proceeding reversing an earlier one.
                                Each event keeps its OWN single direction;
                                the case's ups and downs live in the CHAIN of
                                events (follows/part_of), never in tagging
                                one event Mixed to summarise the whole case.
                              - a procedural side-effect that is not the
                                substance of the claim (costs awarded to a
                                losing party, legal aid granted, a procedural
                                point decided in the applicant's favour while
                                the claim itself is dismissed). Direction
                                tracks the claim or charge being decided, not
                                every incidental ruling inside the decision.
                              - a decision that grants nothing and denies
                                nothing on the merits (remitted, referred
                                onward, left unexamined, inadmissible) — that
                                is Unknown, not Mixed: there is no result to
                                be mixed about.
                            Test: reading only THIS decision, would the
                            applicant say they partly won and partly lost on
                            the same claim? If yes, Mixed. If they simply
                            lost (even with a minor consolation) or simply
                            won (even with a minor qualification), it is not
                            Mixed.
    Unknown                 escape value — including where the direction
                            genuinely cannot be read from the decision, or
                            where nothing was decided on the merits at all
                            (remittal, referral, still pending, inadmissible).
    Read "the applicant" as the future Strasbourg applicant, even where they
    were not yet an applicant at the time.

6.5 type  (subclass of echr:DomesticEvent)
    DomesticProceeding   one set of proceedings before a court or authority
    AdministrativeAction a decision by a state or municipal body outside a
                         formal trial (appointing a guardian, denying a welfare
                         request, refusing a permit)
    EnforcementAction    the physical or bureaucratic execution of an earlier
                         decision (forced relocation, seizure, removal)
    ProsecutorialReview  an internal decision or appeal within a prosecutor's
                         office. Use DomesticProceeding instead once the matter
                         reaches a court.

6.6 authority_kind  (echr:AuthorityKind)
    Judicial        a formal court or tribunal within the national judiciary
    Prosecutorial   a public prosecutor's office or police/investigative body
    Administrative  an executive, municipal, regulatory or welfare authority
    Disciplinary    a professional regulatory body or internal disciplinary board
    Unknown         escape value

6.7 matter  (echr:Matter) -- NOT filled on every event; see §5.1. Not
                              functional -- a list is fine.
    Arrest        the initial act of apprehending or taking into custody
    Detention     an order placing, continuing, extending, reviewing or
                  ending pre-trial/remand detention -- the deprivation of
                  liberty itself; contrast Bail, the decision to release
    Bail          an application for, grant, refusal or review of release
                  from detention pending trial, however the domestic system
                  names the mechanism
    Charge        the formal act of charging or indicting -- contrast
                  Conviction (the trial) and Investigation (what precedes it)
    Conviction    a trial verdict or an appeal against one -- conviction,
                  acquittal, sentence -- ruling on the criminal charge itself
    Investigation an investigative step short of a charge or a detention
                  decision -- a search, a seizure, an expert order, a
                  request refused during an investigation
    Damages       a claim for damages or compensation, and any decision on it
    Custody       custody of or guardianship over A PERSON -- parental
                  custody, a child-abduction return order, legal capacity or
                  guardianship of an adult. NOT detention (that is Detention)
    Employment    a labour dispute -- dismissal, reinstatement, wages, terms
                  of employment
    Pension       a pension, social-security or welfare-benefit entitlement
                  -- grant, refusal, amount, reassessment
    Property      a dispute over property, restitution, tenancy, or a
                  planning/permit decision affecting property
    Extradition   an extradition, deportation or removal decision, or its
                  execution
    LegalAid      an application for, grant of, or refusal of legal aid or
                  legal-aid counsel
    Vetting       granting, refusing or revoking a security clearance or
                  other vetting status
    Disciplinary  a professional or employment disciplinary proceeding --
                  NOT the same axis as authority_kind: Disciplinary there
                  describes the deciding BODY, here it describes the MATTER
    Requests      an application going to the fairness or conduct of the
                  proceeding itself, not to the underlying charge, claim or
                  deprivation of liberty -- recusal of the judge, admission
                  or replacement of counsel, an adjournment, access to the
                  case file or the verbatim record. Petitions are included.
                  Contrast Bail (a request specifically for release from
                  detention, which keeps its own value); a request that is
                  itself about one of this list's other matters stays that
                  matter (an application for damages is Damages, not this)
    Other         the event IS about one identifiable substantive matter,
                  just not one of the above -- not a catch-all for "no
                  substantive matter" (that is an omission, not a value)
    Unknown       clearly about one of these matters, but the text does not
                  say which

6.8 party side  (echr:PartySide)  — written inside `parties`
    Initiating   the party who moved the authority to act at THIS instance:
                 claimant, plaintiff, prosecution, appellant, the party seeking
                 supervisory review or reopening
    Responding   the party the proceeding was brought against at THIS instance:
                 defendant, accused, respondent on appeal
    ThirdParty   joined without being on either side: intervener, civil party
                 joined to a criminal trial, a body appearing to be heard
    Unknown      the facts place the party in the event but not on a side

    Side is per event, not per person. A defendant at trial who appeals is
    Responding in the trial event and Initiating in the appeal event. This is
    the whole reason participation is a node rather than a pair of columns.

================================================================================
7. WHEN TO WRITE "Unknown" AND WHEN TO OMIT
================================================================================

OMIT the field entirely when the text gives you nothing and the vocabulary has
no escape value — dates, quotes, names.

WRITE the escape value (Unknown) when the field has a closed vocabulary, the
event exists, and the text does not tell you which value applies. Unknown is a
real answer meaning "the document does not say".

NEVER GUESS from background knowledge of the legal system. If the judgment does
not say the Regional Court sat on appeal, level is Unknown even where you know
Bulgarian regional courts usually do. You are annotating the document, not the
jurisdiction.

================================================================================
8. IDENTITY — WHEN ARE TWO MENTIONS THE SAME EVENT
================================================================================

You will need this when the judgment revisits an event later, and it is what
fixes the granularity of the whole annotation.

Two mentions are the SAME event when they share the same deciding authority and
the same decision. In practice:

  - Same authority, same decision date  ->  same event.
  - Naming variants are the same authority: "the Regional Court", "the Sofia
    Regional Court" and "the Sofia City Regional Court" are one body where the
    text is plainly referring to the same one.
  - Same authority, different dates  ->  different events. Under §3.1 the
    institution and the decision of one proceeding ARE two events, so this no
    longer carries an exception.
  - A date given only as a year matches a full date in that year where the
    authority and the surrounding narrative agree.

FILING EVENTS (§3.1) have no decision to match on, so match them on authority
and start_date instead:

  - Same authority, same start_date, neither carrying a decision_date  ->  same
    filing event.
  - A filing event NEVER matches a decision event, even for the same authority
    and the same proceeding. They are the two halves of a split pair and the
    alignment must keep them apart, or the split collapses at scoring time.

Where you genuinely cannot tell, record one event and say so in `note`.
