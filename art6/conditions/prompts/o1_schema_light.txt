You are analysing a judgment of the European Court of Human Rights. Your task is to extract the chain of DOMESTIC legal proceedings the case passed through before it reached Strasbourg. The goal is to be able to reconstruct the timeline of key events and legal procedures that led up to the case reaching the European Court.

WHAT COUNTS

Extract every domestic step that directly affected the applicant in THIS case, and take the range of them seriously. A chain is not only court hearings. It also includes:

  - decisions by a state or municipal body that is not a court -- appointing a guardian, refusing a welfare request, placing someone in an institution;
  - the enforcement of an earlier decision -- bailiffs, officials, the bureaucratic execution of a judgment;
  - decisions taken inside a prosecutor's office that never reach a court -- a refusal to open an investigation, an internal appeal against that refusal.

These are the easiest steps to miss, precisely because they are not court hearings, and a chain containing only the courtroom steps is incomplete. Do not force every entry into the shape of a court case just because that is the most familiar kind.

Two exclusions, both important:
  - Nothing that happened at the European Court of Human Rights itself. The chain ends at the last domestic decision.
  - No other cases. Judgments frequently cite decisions from unrelated cases as legal authority ("as the Court held in X v. Y", "cited as a precedent"). Those are not steps in this applicant's proceedings, however decision-like they read.

WHAT IS ONE ENTRY

Each entry is ONE domestic event -- one act by one body, on its own date. What counts as an
event, and how finely to split, is defined in WHAT TO EXTRACT below (COVERAGE AND
GRANULARITY): a filing narrated as its own act and the decision that disposes of it are TWO
entries; a trial's procedural rulings are not entries at all; each investigative act -- a
search, an arrest, the opening of proceedings -- is its own entry. Those definitions are the
same ones every other extraction in this study follows, and they decide every boundary case.

Do not merge distinct bodies. Two different courts, or a court and the prosecutor's office, are never the same entry even when they dealt with the same matter.

Judgments often describe several proceedings running in PARALLEL -- a criminal prosecution and a civil damages claim over the same events, or an applicant's own appeal alongside the State's. Extract every track. Order the entries by date across all tracks; do not drop one track because another is more prominent.

FIELDS

Return a JSON object with these keys. Use null for anything the document does not state.
Do not guess, and do not leave a field out because you are unsure -- an explicit null is a
finding, a missing key is not.

"case": {"case_name", "application_number", "respondent_state", "judgment_date"} -- as the
judgment's heading gives them; dates as ISO.

"applicants": a list of {"name", "birth_year", "nationality", "residence", "gender"} -- as
the document describes the applicants themselves.

"persons": a list of {"name", "role", "gender", "represents"} -- every person the document
names in the domestic proceedings (applicants, lawyers, guardians, relatives, witnesses,
co-accused), never a judge. `gender` only where an honorific, pronoun or gendered noun
marks it; `represents` the name of the person they act for, if any.

"proceedings": an array, one element per entry, each with exactly these keys:

  "order"             1-based position, earliest first, counting across all parallel tracks.
  "follows"           A list of the "order" values of the earlier entries this one continues,
                      reviews, appeals or enforces as a NEW proceeding -- an appeal names the
                      judgment it appeals; a cassation ruling names that appeal; an
                      enforcement step names the judgment enforced. Usually one value. null
                      where the entry begins a track, or where `part_of` or `occurs_during`
                      applies instead. Not chronology: that is already in "order".
  "part_of"           The "order" of the EARLIEST entry of the same proceeding, where this
                      entry is a further act of it -- same body, same rung, same procedure:
                      a decision disposing of a filing narrated as its own entry. null
                      otherwise.
  "occurs_during"     The "order" of a proceeding that was still pending when this one began
                      and was decided -- an interim ruling inside a main claim, a detention
                      review during a trial. null otherwise.
  "deciding_body"     The court, tribunal, prosecutor's office or administrative body that
                      decided this entry, as the document names it. The BODY, never the
                      individual judge.
  "authority_kind"    judicial | prosecutorial | administrative | disciplinary | unknown.
  "decision_date"     ISO date, YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYY-MM / YYYY where that is all the document
                      gives. Copy the date stated; do not compute one.
  "start_date"        The date it was begun or lodged, if separately given, same format.
  "instance_level"    One of the values in WHAT TO EXTRACT: first instance | appeal |
                      cassation | investigative | administrative review | case review |
                      reopening | unknown.
  "outcome"           One of: merits decided | inadmissible | remitted | settled or
                      withdrawn | other -- or null where the entry decided nothing (an
                      arrest, a search), as WHAT TO EXTRACT defines.
  "proceeding_type"   criminal | civil | administrative | constitutional | disciplinary |
                      other | unknown.
  "outcome_direction" favourable to applicant | unfavourable to applicant | mixed | unknown
                      -- only where the entry has an outcome.
  "matter"            A list of zero or more matter values as WHAT TO EXTRACT defines them.
                      Not on every entry.
  "is_pending"        true where the document says this was still pending; otherwise null.
  "is_final"          true on the entry that concluded its chain of domestic proceedings,
                      where the document says it was final or remedies were exhausted.
  "is_non_occurring"  true where this is something a party did NOT do ("did not appeal").
  "supporting_quote"  A span copied EXACTLY from the document, character for character, that
                      evidences this entry. Do not paraphrase, tidy punctuation or stitch two
                      passages together. If you cannot find one, null.
  "parties"           A list of the parties to THIS entry and their side, written
                      "name (initiating)", "name (responding)" or "name (third party)". Roles
                      are per entry: who brought this one and who answered it. Never the
                      deciding body, never a lawyer in place of the client, never a judge.
  "custodial_measure" Any pre-trial deprivation of liberty ordered, extended or ended in this
                      entry -- remand, police custody, psychiatric confinement -- with its
                      dates where the document gives them.

OUTPUT

The JSON object and nothing else. No commentary before or after it, no markdown code fence.
