You are analysing a judgment of the European Court of Human Rights. Your task is to extract the chain of DOMESTIC legal proceedings the case passed through before it reached Strasbourg. The goal is to be able to reconstruct the timeline of key events and legal procedures that led up to the case reaching the European Court.

WHAT COUNTS

Extract every domestic step that directly affected the applicant in THIS case, and take the range of them seriously. A chain is not only court hearings. It also includes:

  - decisions by a state or municipal body that is not a court -- appointing a guardian, refusing a welfare request, placing someone in an institution;
  - the enforcement of an earlier decision -- bailiffs, officials, the bureaucratic execution of a judgment;
  - decisions taken inside a prosecutor's office that never reach a court -- a refusal to open an investigation, an internal appeal against that refusal.

These are the easiest steps to miss, precisely because they are not court hearings, and a chain containing only the courtroom steps is incomplete. Do not force every entry into the shape of a court case just because that is the most familiar kind.

Two exclusions, both important:
  - Nothing that happened at the European Court of Human Rights itself. The chain ends at the last domestic decision.
  - No other cases. Judgments frequently cite decisions from unrelated cases as legal authority ("as the Court held in X v. Y", "cited as a precedent"). Those are not steps in this applicant's proceedings, however decision-like they read.

ONE ENTRY PER INSTANCE -- AND AN INSTANCE IS NOT A HEARING

Each entry is exactly ONE step decided by ONE body on ONE date. A single entry must never carry two deciding bodies or two decision dates. When a matter is appealed, reaches a different court, or receives a further decision on a different date, that is a NEW entry -- never a modification of the earlier one.

But an "instance" means one JURISDICTIONAL LEVEL -- one passage of the matter before one body, ending in that body's decision. It does not mean every ruling that body made along the way. A single first-instance trial may involve a dozen procedural decisions: rulings on the admissibility of evidence, refusals to stay proceedings, grants and withdrawals of bail, adjournments, acquittals on individual counts. **All of those belong to ONE entry -- the trial -- not to twelve.** Record the trial, its deciding body and its outcome. The same applies at every level.

Two failure modes, and they pull in opposite directions:
  - Collapsing a whole appellate history into one entry per "case". Wrong: a first-instance judgment, an appeal and a cassation ruling are three entries.
  - Splitting one instance into one entry per procedural ruling. Also wrong: a trial that took nine months and thirty rulings is still one entry.

Do not merge distinct bodies. Two different courts, or a court and the prosecutor's office, are never the same entry even when they dealt with the same matter.

Judgments often describe several proceedings running in PARALLEL -- a criminal prosecution and a civil damages claim over the same events, or an applicant's own appeal alongside the State's. Extract every track. Order the entries by date across all tracks; do not drop one track because another is more prominent.

FIELDS

Return a JSON object with a single key "proceedings", whose value is an array. Each element of that array is one proceeding, with exactly these keys:

  "order"             1-based position in the chain, earliest first, counting across all parallel tracks.
  "follows"           A list of the "order" values of the earlier entries this step directly reviews or continues -- an appeal names the judgment it appeals; a cassation ruling names that appeal; an enforcement step names the judgment being enforced. Usually one value. Give several only where a single decision disposes of two earlier ones at once. Use null where the step begins a track rather than continuing one, and note that a document often contains SEVERAL independent tracks, each starting with its own null. Do not link two steps merely because one happened after the other: this field records review and continuation, not chronology, and the chronology is already in "order".
  "deciding_body"     The court, tribunal, prosecutor's office or administrative body that decided this step, named as the document names it ("the Ruse Regional Court", "the Constitutional Court", "the district prosecutor"). Name the BODY, not the individual judge presiding over it. Use null if the document does not say.
  "decision_date"     ISO date, YYYY-MM-DD. Where the document gives only a month or year, use YYYY-MM or YYYY. Use null if the document does not say. Copy the date the document states; do not compute or infer one.
  "instance_level"    Where this step sits in the hierarchy, in your own words -- for example first instance, appeal, cassation, constitutional review, investigative, administrative review, supervisory review, reopening. Use null if you cannot tell.
  "outcome"           What the body actually decided, in your own words -- the claim was dismissed, the appeal was allowed, the case was remitted to the lower court, the complaint was declared inadmissible, the investigation was discontinued, proceedings are still pending. Use null if the document does not say.
  "supporting_quote"  A span copied EXACTLY from the document, character for character, that evidences this proceeding. Do not paraphrase, reword, tidy punctuation, modernise spelling, or stitch together text from two separate passages. If you cannot find a span that supports the entry, use null rather than approximating one -- an unevidenced entry is recoverable, a falsely evidenced one is not.
  "parties"           A list of the individuals or bodies participating in this specific step, and which side they were on (e.g., "applicant (appellant)", "State (respondent)", "prosecutor (initiating)"). Do not merge roles across different instances; record only their role at this exact stage. Use null if the document does not say.
  "custodial_measure" Any deprivation of liberty or legal capacity restriction explicitly applied or maintained during this step (e.g., police custody, remand in custody, psychiatric confinement). Use null if the document does not mention one.Use null for anything the document does not state. Do not guess, and do not leave a field out because you are unsure -- an explicit null is a finding, a missing key is not.

OUTPUT

The JSON object and nothing else. No commentary before or after it, no markdown code fence.
