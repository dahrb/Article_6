You are reading the opening of a European Court of Human Rights judgment or decision.

Your ONLY job is to find how the document describes the applicants themselves, and to copy
what it says. Ignore the procedural history, the domestic courts, the complaints and the
law. You are not summarising the case.

Return JSON and nothing else:

{
  "applicants": [
    {
      "name_span": "this applicant as the document names them",
      "birth_year_span": "the words giving THIS applicant's year of birth",
      "nationality_span": "the words giving THIS applicant's nationality",
      "residence_span": "the words saying where THIS applicant lives",
      "description_span": "the sentence the fields above were read from, copied whole"
    }
  ]
}

EVERY VALUE IS COPIED FROM THE TEXT, character for character. Never paraphrase, never
normalise, never translate, never reconstruct. If the document does not state a field,
leave that field out entirely — a missing field is correct and an invented one is not.

The description is nearly always one sentence near the start of the facts:

    "The applicant, Mr Nikolay Vitruk, is a Ukrainian national who was born in 1979 and
     lives in Kiev."
    "The applicant was born in 1954 and lives in Diyarbakir."
    "Mr Karlheinz Beer is a German national, born in 1952 and resident in Darmstadt."
    "The applicant is a Polish national, born in 1921 and currently serving a sentence of
     life imprisonment in HM Prison Gartree."

The birth year counts in EVERY wording — "was born in", "who was born in", and the bare
participial "born in 1952 and resident in ..." alike. It still counts when a place follows
it: in "born in 1956 in Ruse", 1956 is the birth year and Ruse is NOT the residence.
Residence is what the document says the person lives in or is currently held in.

ONE ENTRY PER APPLICANT. "The applicants", "M.N. and Others", "the applicant and his wife"
are several people, and each gets an entry. Where the document lists values for several of
them — "born in 1933, 1938, 1952, 1948 and 1945 respectively" — each value belongs to the
applicant in the matching position, and you copy that applicant's own value, not the whole
list. Where the counts do not line up (five applicants, two places of residence), omit
that field rather than guessing which applicant it belongs to.

Where the document NEVER separates the applicants — "a married couple and their two minor
children, all Syrian nationals", with no names and no individual values — return ONE entry
whose `name_span` copies that collective description. Do not invent separate people the
text does not distinguish, and do not put a shared nationality on one of them as if it
were theirs alone.

Applicants only. Do not describe lawyers, agents, judges, witnesses or relatives, however
fully the opening describes them. Where the applicant is a company or public body ("Scholz
AG"), give its entry a `name_span` and a `description_span` and leave the birth year,
nationality and residence out — those belong to human beings.
