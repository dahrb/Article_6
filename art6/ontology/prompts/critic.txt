TASK: You are the CRITIC. You are given a European Court of Human Rights judgment and a
DRAFT of its domestic procedural history: the events extracted from it, the tracks they
were grouped into, and the link from each event to the one it continues. Find what is
WRONG with the draft. You do not fix anything -- a later pass does that, from your
findings alone. A problem you do not report will not be fixed.

Read the judgment against the draft, paragraph by paragraph. Report a finding for each of:

  missing_event    a domestic act the judgment narrates, with its own date, that no draft
                   event records. Look hardest at the paragraphs listed under CHECK THESE.
  duplicate_event  two draft events that record the SAME act (same body, same date, same
                   decision). Two acts by one body on one day are NOT duplicates -- a
                   search, an arrest and the opening of proceedings are three acts.
  not_an_event     a draft event that is not a domestic act concerning these applicants:
                   a Strasbourg step, a precedent from another case, a statute, trial
                   management (see COVERAGE AND GRANULARITY).
  wrong_field      a classification (`instance_level`, `outcome`, `proceeding_type`,
                   `outcome_direction`, `matter`) or a span (a date, the authority) that
                   the judgment contradicts. Name the field.
  missing_link     an event that plainly continues an earlier draft event but has no link.
  wrong_link       a link to the wrong predecessor, or of the wrong kind (`continues`
                   where the predecessor was still pending, or the reverse).
  wrong_track      an event grouped with a matter it does not continue -- above all two
                   PARALLEL tracks (civil and criminal over the same facts, proceedings
                   against a co-accused, a new matter arising later) chained into one.

Every finding needs `evidence_span`: words copied VERBATIM from the judgment that show
the problem -- the sentence narrating the missing act, the words stating the true outcome,
the words showing what an appeal was against. For `duplicate_event` and `not_an_event`
it may instead be the draft event's own quoted words. A finding whose evidence cannot be
found in the judgment is discarded unread.

Report problems, not preferences. If the draft's reading is defensible on the judgment's
words, leave it. Do not report a field the judgment does not settle either way. Fewer
correct findings are worth more than many doubtful ones; never more than 40.

The draft follows the extraction rules below. Hold it to them -- they define what counts
as an event and how each classification is decided.

## OUTPUT

A single JSON object, no prose around it. An empty list is a correct answer for a good
draft.

{
  "findings": [
    {"id": "f1",
     "kind": "missing_event | duplicate_event | not_an_event | wrong_field | missing_link |
              wrong_link | wrong_track",
     "events": ["e3"],
     "field": "outcome  (wrong_field only)",
     "evidence_span": "verbatim words from the judgment",
     "issue": "one sentence: what is wrong and what the judgment shows instead"}
  ]
}
