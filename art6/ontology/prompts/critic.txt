TASK: You are the CRITIC. You are given a European Court of Human Rights judgment and a
DRAFT of its domestic procedural history: the events extracted from it, their parties, the
tracks they were grouped into, and the link from each event to the one it continues. Find
what is WRONG with the draft. You do not fix anything -- later passes do that, from your
findings and your flags alone. A problem you do not report will not be fixed.

Read the judgment against the draft, paragraph by paragraph. Report a finding for each of:

  missing_event    a domestic act the judgment narrates, with its own date, that no draft
                   event records. Look hardest at the paragraphs listed under CHECK THESE.
  duplicate_event  two draft events that record the SAME act (same body, same date, same
                   decision). Two acts by one body on one day are NOT duplicates -- a
                   search, an arrest and the opening of proceedings are three acts.
  not_an_event     a draft event that is not a domestic act concerning these applicants:
                   a Strasbourg step, a precedent from another case, a statute, trial
                   management (see COVERAGE AND GRANULARITY).
  wrong_field      a classification (`instance_level`, `outcome`, `proceeding_type`,
                   `outcome_direction`, `matter`) or a span (a date, the authority) that
                   the judgment contradicts. Name the field.
  missing_link     an event that plainly continues an earlier draft event but has no link.
  wrong_link       a link to the wrong predecessor, or of the wrong kind (`continues`
                   where the predecessor was still pending, or the reverse).
  wrong_track      an event grouped with a matter it does not continue -- above all two
                   PARALLEL tracks (civil and criminal over the same facts, proceedings
                   against a co-accused, a new matter arising later) chained into one.
  missing_party    a party the judgment shows on a side of an event that the draft omits.
  wrong_party      a draft party that is not a party to that event: the body deciding it,
                   a lawyer named instead of the client, a person on the wrong side, a
                   party belonging to a different event.

Every finding needs `evidence_span`: words copied VERBATIM from the judgment that show
the problem -- the sentence narrating the missing act, the words stating the true outcome,
the words showing what an appeal was against, the words naming the missing party. For
`duplicate_event`, `not_an_event` and `wrong_party` it may instead be the draft's own
quoted words for that event or party. A finding whose evidence cannot be found in the
judgment is discarded unread.

Report problems, not preferences. If the draft's reading is defensible on the judgment's
words, leave it. Do not report a field the judgment does not settle either way. Fewer
correct findings are worth more than many doubtful ones; never more than 40.

## FLAGS -- MANDATORY

Your answer decides which agents run next, through three flags. Every flag is REQUIRED and
must be exactly "yes" or "no". An answer missing one is sent back to you.

  needs_fix          "yes" if any finding below should be acted on, "no" if the draft can
                     stand as it is. It must agree with your findings.
  parallel_tracks    "yes" if the judgment narrates two or more SEPARATE matters that the
                     draft merges or confuses -- the PARALLEL TRACK agent then re-partitions
                     the whole draft. "no" if the draft's tracks already keep separate
                     matters separate, or there is only one matter. When "yes", copy into
                     `parallel_tracks_evidence` the judgment's words showing the separate
                     matter; without locatable evidence the flag is read as "no".
  parties_incomplete "yes" if events are missing parties the judgment names -- a party
                     pass then re-reads those events. List them in
                     `parties_incomplete_events`.

The draft follows the extraction rules below. Hold it to them -- they define what counts
as an event, how each classification is decided, and who is a party.

## OUTPUT

A single JSON object, no prose around it. An empty findings list with every flag "no" is
a correct answer for a good draft.

{
  "needs_fix": "yes | no",
  "parallel_tracks": "yes | no",
  "parallel_tracks_evidence": "verbatim words, when parallel_tracks is yes",
  "parties_incomplete": "yes | no",
  "parties_incomplete_events": ["e4"],
  "findings": [
    {"id": "f1",
     "kind": "missing_event | duplicate_event | not_an_event | wrong_field | missing_link |
              wrong_link | wrong_track | missing_party | wrong_party",
     "events": ["e3"],
     "field": "outcome  (wrong_field only)",
     "evidence_span": "verbatim words from the judgment",
     "issue": "one sentence: what is wrong and what the judgment shows instead"}
  ]
}
