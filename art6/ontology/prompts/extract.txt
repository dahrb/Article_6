TASK: Read this European Court of Human Rights judgment and SELECT THE EVIDENCE for the
domestic procedural history — everything that happened in the respondent State before the
case reached Strasbourg.

You are a SELECTOR, not a summariser and not a classifier.

## THE ONE RULE

Every value you output must be text COPIED CHARACTER FOR CHARACTER from the judgment.

Not paraphrased, not tidied, not re-punctuated, not stitched together from two places. If
you cannot copy it exactly, leave the field out. An absent field is recoverable; a field
holding text that is not in the document is not, because it looks exactly like a fact.

Keep each span SHORT — the few words that carry the fact, long enough to be unambiguous if
the same words appear elsewhere.

## WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO

Do not decide what category anything belongs to, except the three judgement fields named
below (`outcome`, `instance_level`, `matter`). You do not say whether a party initiated or
responded, whether a court is judicial or administrative, whether an outcome was
favourable. A later stage decides that from the words you copy.

Do not normalise dates. Copy "20 November 2000" as it appears, not "2000-11-20".

Do not model the Strasbourg proceedings, other cases cited as precedent, or statutory
provisions. Only what happened to THESE applicants in the domestic system — including
non-judicial steps: administrative decisions, prosecutorial rulings, enforcement actions.

domestic step concerning these applicants at all, and then `events` is empty. Later passes
are skipped on a "no".

## CLASSIFY THE RUNG AND THE RESULT

These come in PAIRS, and the pairing is the point:

  instance_level / outcome      a judgement you make, filled on EVERY event
  instance_span / outcome_span  the literal words, ONLY when the document states them

Everything else here is copied text; these are the exception, because they are judgements
ABOUT an event rather than phrases in it. The evidence for them is the event's own
`what_happened_span`, which anyone can check the judgement against.

Never withhold the classification because there is no span. The judgment usually will not
write "first instance" or "cassation" anywhere, and waiting for those words leaves the
field empty on almost every event. Span or no span, the classification is filled.

Which rung:

  - a first hearing of a claim, charge or application  -> first instance
  - a review of a lower body's decision on the merits  -> appeal
  - a review limited to points of law, or ANY ruling by a supreme or constitutional
    court on a case brought up to it                   -> cassation
  - a step inside a criminal investigation (a warrant, an arrest, a
    prosecutor's refusal, an expert order)             -> investigative
  - ANY decision by an administrative body -- a ministry, office, agency, council,
    mayor, fund, welfare department, board of a public body -- whether it decides
    first or reviews its own earlier decision         -> administrative review
  - a review of an already-decided stage by a body outside the
    ordinary appeal ladder (an extraordinary review after finality)  -> case review
  - a case reopened after a final decision             -> reopening
  - nothing was heard or reviewed at all -- a release, a payment, a transfer between
    institutions, an agreement between private parties -> unknown

Classify by what the body DID in this case, never by its name. A tribunal or court called
an "Appeals Board" or "Court of Appeal" that is the FIRST court to hear a challenge to an
administrative decision is first instance; it is appeal only when it reviews another
court's ruling. Where the procedure is unclear, follow the chain: the first court to hear
the matter is first instance, the court reviewing it is appeal, the highest court is
cassation.

A filing takes the rung of the proceeding it institutes: an indictment or a claim lodged
with a trial court is first instance, an appeal lodged is appeal. Both acts of one
proceeding -- the filing and its decision -- carry the same rung.

Which result:

  - the body ruled on the substance, whichever way      -> merits decided
  - a claim or appeal refused without reaching merits   -> inadmissible
  - sent back to a lower body                           -> remitted
  - withdrawn, discontinued or settled                  -> settled or withdrawn
  - a real outcome none of these fit                    -> other

These five are the whole vocabulary. There is no value for "an order to do something": a
body that GRANTS a request -- appointing a guardian, allowing an injunction, ordering a
release -- has ruled on the substance of it, so it is `merits decided`. A housekeeping
order deciding nothing, such as a transfer on jurisdictional grounds, is `other`.

`other` is a last resort, not a default. A decision to leave a claim unexamined is
`inadmissible`, not `other`; a request refused during an investigation is `inadmissible`,
not `merits decided`.

**AN ACT IS NOT A RESULT. Leave `outcome` OUT when nothing was decided.** A gun is seized,
a warrant is issued, a suspect is arrested, an investigation is opened: things DONE, not
requests answered. `merits decided` is false (no merits were reached) and `other` is false
(there was no result at all) -- omit the field. `instance_level` is unaffected: classify
the rung as always. The test is whether something was ASKED and ANSWERED; an arrest answers
nothing. This is where the habit of filling every field does most damage: a wrong outcome
is a gap nobody can see, an omitted one is a gap anyone can.

## CLASSIFY THE TRACK AND THE DIRECTION

Two more judgements, same treatment as `instance_level` and `outcome` above: no span
required, evidenced by the event's own `what_happened_span`.

`proceeding_type` -- the branch of law, filled on EVERY event, same test regardless of
result or rung:

  - a criminal charge, conviction, sentence, arrest, detention, prosecution   -> criminal
  - a civil claim between private parties, damages, contract, family, property -> civil
  - a decision by a state body outside a court, a licence, a benefit, a
    disciplinary or vetting body, a public-law claim against the state       -> administrative
  - a challenge to a law's or measure's constitutionality                    -> constitutional
  - a professional or judicial disciplinary proceeding                      -> disciplinary
  - a real branch none of these fit                                          -> other
  - not enough said to tell                                                  -> unknown

`outcome_direction` -- did THIS decision come out for or against the future Strasbourg
applicant. Filled under the SAME condition as `outcome`: an event with no outcome (an act,
not a result) has no direction either, and both are omitted together.

  - the claim granted, the charge not made out, the appeal that sought
    this result succeeded, in full                          -> favourable to applicant
  - the claim dismissed, the charge made out, the appeal that
    sought this result failed, in full                       -> unfavourable to applicant
  - THIS decision itself grants something AND denies something on the
    SAME claim (a conviction with a reduced sentence on appeal, damages
    awarded below what was sought)                            -> mixed
  - remitted, referred onward, left unexamined, inadmissible -- nothing
    was granted or denied on the merits, so there is nothing to be
    favourable, unfavourable or mixed ABOUT                   -> unknown

Direction tracks the claim or charge actually being decided, not every incidental ruling
inside the decision (legal aid granted alongside a claim that is dismissed is still
unfavourable, not mixed).

## CLASSIFY THE MATTER

`matter` says what an event was substantively ABOUT. It is a DIFFERENT AXIS from
`proceeding_type_span` (the track -- civil, criminal, administrative): bail and detention
are both criminal, usually decided by the same court in the same case, and substantively
nothing alike. `matter` is what tells them apart.

UNLIKE `outcome` and `instance_level`, `matter` is NOT filled on every event. An appeal, a
cassation review, a remittal are not substantively about any of these values -- leave it
out entirely. Fill it only where the event's own content is recognisably one of:

  arrest        the initial act of apprehending or taking into custody
  detention     placing, continuing, extending, reviewing or ending pre-trial/remand
                detention -- the deprivation of liberty itself; contrast bail
  bail          application for, grant, refusal or review of release pending trial
  charge        the formal act of charging or indicting
  conviction    a trial verdict or an appeal against one -- conviction, acquittal,
                sentence -- ruling on the criminal charge itself
  investigation an investigative step short of a charge or detention decision -- A SEARCH,
                A SEIZURE, an expert order, a request refused during an investigation
  damages       a claim for damages or compensation, and any decision on it
  custody       custody of or guardianship over A PERSON -- parental custody, a
                child-abduction return order, OR LEGAL CAPACITY / GUARDIANSHIP OF AN ADULT
                (appointing a guardian, restoring or refusing to restore legal capacity).
                NOT detention.
  employment    a labour dispute -- dismissal, reinstatement, wages, terms of employment
  pension       a pension, social-security or welfare-benefit entitlement
  property      a dispute over property, restitution, tenancy, or a planning/permit
                decision affecting property
  extradition   an extradition, deportation or removal decision, or its execution
  legal aid     application for, grant of, or refusal of legal aid or legal-aid counsel
  requests      an application going to the fairness or conduct of the proceeding itself,
                not to the underlying charge, claim or detention -- recusal of the judge,
                admission or replacement of counsel, an adjournment, access to the case file
                or the verbatim record; petitions. A request for release stays `bail`; a
                request about one of the other matters keeps that matter.
  vetting       granting, refusing or revoking a security clearance or vetting status
  disciplinary  A PROFESSIONAL OR EMPLOYMENT DISCIPLINARY PROCEEDING -- suspending,
                revoking or restoring a licence to practise, a bar/medical/police ruling
  other         escape value, see below
  unknown       escape value, see below

CHECK THIS LIST BEFORE REACHING FOR `other`. Measured misses, all tagged `other` when the
list above names them in so many words: "a gun was seized" and "a search warrant was
issued" (= investigation); "appointed ... as the applicant's guardian" and "refused to
bring an action for restoration of ... legal capacity" (= custody); "suspended the
applicant's licence to practise as an advocate" (= disciplinary). Read the event's content
and find the value it matches BEFORE concluding none fits.

Give MORE THAN ONE value where one event genuinely rules on more than one matter -- an
order continuing detention while setting bail conditions is `["detention", "bail"]`.

`other` and `unknown` are for an event that IS about a specific matter: `other` where you
can identify it but the list does not name it (checked, not assumed), `unknown` where the
text signals a substantive matter you cannot pin down. Neither is a default for an event
with no substantive matter at all -- a costs order, a housekeeping ruling, a step about no
identifiable substance gets NO `matter` field, the same way an act with no result gets no
`outcome`.

## COVERAGE AND GRANULARITY

ONE ENTRY PER JURISDICTIONAL INSTANCE — not one per procedural act. But an instance is TWO
entries, not one, where the judgment NARRATES the lodging as its own act, separately from
the decision -- see NARRATED VS REPORTED below for exactly when that applies and how to
link the two. Everything else here still holds: it is one INSTANCE either way, and what
changes is only whether that instance is written as one entry carrying two dates or as two
linked entries each carrying one.

Where the filing is not separately narrated: lodging date in `start_date_span`, decision
date in `decision_date_span`, on the SAME entry. Everything in between before the same body
— an amendment, a counterclaim, a request, a transfer — belongs to that same entry, in
`interim_spans`. So a claim lodged in January, amended in October, met with a counterclaim
and left unexamined in November by the same court is ONE entry, not five.

A NEW entry begins when:
  - a DIFFERENT body takes the matter (appeal, cassation, transfer to another court), or
  - the same body decides a genuinely separate matter on its own facts, or
  - the filing is narrated as its own act -- see NARRATED VS REPORTED.

Include instances that went nowhere — a request refused, an appeal declared inadmissible, a
claim left unexamined. Include non-judicial ones: administrative decisions, prosecutorial
rulings, enforcement steps.

RECORD EVERY INSTANCE THAT QUALIFIES, ESPECIALLY IN A LONG RUN OF SIMILAR-LOOKING ONES. A
document that narrates the same body deciding many times in sequence (a fund, a board, an
agency, ruling repeatedly over several years) is not a case for extracting only the
"important" few -- each dated act is its own entry on the same terms as any other. The
asymmetry is what matters: an entry you wrote and should not have is visible and can be
removed later; an entry you never wrote is invisible and nothing downstream can recover it.
When in doubt about whether something in a dense sequence counts, write the entry.

A DECISION DOES NOT NEED A DECIDING VERB. "Dismissed", "granted", "quashed" are the easy
cases. A body just as clearly decides something when the sentence reports what it FOUND,
CONCLUDED or DETERMINED, even mid-paragraph and even where that finding is only one clause
of a longer sentence giving background: "the Fund concluded that X had failed to meet its
obligations, rendering the agreement no longer effective" is a decision -- the Fund decided
the agreement's status -- exactly as much as if the sentence had said "the Fund declared the
agreement void". Read for WHETHER SOMETHING WAS SETTLED, not for a specific verb.

### A SHARED DISPOSAL SENTENCE DOES NOT MEAN A SHARED ENTRY

"Both requests were rejected by the Regional Court", answering two requests about different
subjects, is TWO entries -- even though one sentence disposes of both and is the only date
either gets. Ask what each request was ABOUT, not how the ruling is reported: a motion to
disqualify the judge for bias and a motion to admit a different lawyer are separate matters
on their own facts. Worked example -- "the applicant's civil defender requested the Regional
Court to dismiss the judge presiding in the case for bias... On the same day the applicant
requested to admit Mrs Moskalenko as his lawyer... Both requests were rejected":

  entry A: institution_narrated_span "requested ... to dismiss the judge ... for bias",
  decision_date_span "19 May", outcome_span "Both requests were rejected", party: the
  civil defender.
  entry B: institution_narrated_span "requested to admit Mrs Moskalenko as his lawyer",
  the SAME date and the SAME outcome span, party: the applicant.

Sharing a date and an outcome span across two entries is correct here, not duplication to
avoid. One entry with both requests folded into its parties list is the wrong shape: it has
to fit a single `outcome`/`matter` that cannot be true of two different matters at once.

The same applies to a court's own further rulings issued alongside a judgment the same day
(further orders on the conduct of the investigation, issued the same day as a conviction,
are a separate entry from the conviction even though only the date links them).

### WHAT NOT TO RECORD: TRIAL MANAGEMENT

Not everything asked and answered during a hearing is its own entry. Record a ruling made
DURING an already-open trial only if it did one of these:

  - authorised or ended a deprivation of liberty (bail granted, withdrawn or reinstated,
    detention ordered or extended) -- always record this
  - created, removed or altered a right independent of the trial's own merits (refusing to
    admit chosen defence counsel, disqualifying the judge for bias, refusing legal aid)
  - disposed of the case or a charge in it (a directed acquittal, a severance removing a
    defendant or a count entirely)

An ordinary ruling on the conduct of a hearing in progress -- admitting or excluding
evidence, refusing to sever counts, ruling there is a case to answer, refusing to extend
time for a submission -- is trial management, not an event. Leave it out entirely; do not
put it in `interim_spans` either, which is for steps in a proceeding still being decided,
not the internal mechanics of a trial under way.

A REFUSED APPLICATION ABOUT THE CONDUCT OF THE TRIAL IS STILL TRIAL MANAGEMENT. Refusing to
stay the trial as an abuse of process, refusing to sever, ruling evidence admissible: they
are answered, and still not events. Trial management does not become an event by losing.

AND A RULING THAT DOES DISPOSE OF A CHARGE STILL NEEDS ITS OWN DATE. "At the close of the
prosecution case, the judge ruled ... that the applicant should be acquitted [on two of
four counts]" names no date of its own, so it is not a separate entry. DO NOT SEARCH
ELSEWHERE IN THE DOCUMENT FOR ONE: a trial's own start or end date, quoted from a different
sentence ("the trial which took place between 8 February and 1 April 1999"), is not this
ruling's date merely because it is nearest. `decision_date_span` must be words FROM THE
SENTENCE DESCRIBING THIS RULING, or omitted -- borrowing manufactures a fact the document
does not state, even though the borrowed text is itself a real span from somewhere.

THE SAME RULE APPLIES WHEN A BODY'S ACT IS NARRATED MORE THAN ONCE. A document that
describes the same kind of body reaching the same kind of result at two different points --
once for each of two separate chains, or once in passing and once in full -- is not
evidence they are one event, and each mention keeps ITS OWN date and quote from ITS OWN
sentence. Before treating two mentions as duplicates of a single act, check what each one
continues: if they continue different earlier entries, they are different entries, however
similar the wording. Never let one mention's date attach to the other's entry because the
two sentences look alike -- that is the same borrowing error as above, just between two
narrated events instead of between a ruling and the trial around it.

THIS IS A RULE ABOUT WHICH DATE GOES WHERE, NOT A REASON TO WRITE FEWER ENTRIES. It does not
touch the separate question of a request and its own outcome ("she requested an adjournment"
/ "her request was not granted"), or of two requests answered in one sentence (see A SHARED
DISPOSAL SENTENCE above) -- those are still always their own entries. When genuinely unsure
whether two mentions are the same act or two, the existing rule still holds: an extra entry
is visible and removable, a missing one is not, so write both.

A FILING NARRATED SEPARATELY FROM ITS OWN DECISION IS NOT THIS CASE EITHER, however similar
the two sentences sound -- that is NARRATED VS REPORTED (below), a different rule, and it
still means TWO entries every time it applies. This paragraph is about two mentions of what
might be the SAME act; a request and the ruling that answers it are never the same act.

### NARRATED VS REPORTED

Say whether the judgment gave the filing its own narrated act or merely reported it, and
this now decides whether you write ONE entry or TWO:

  NARRATED: "On 15 March 1999 the applicant instituted civil proceedings against his
  employer... On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed the claim." Two sentences, two acts ->
  TWO ENTRIES. Entry A (the filing): start_date_span "15 March 1999",
  institution_narrated_span the filing sentence, NO decision_date_span, NO outcome.
  Entry B (the decision): decision_date_span "5 June 2003", the outcome, NO
  start_date_span. Write both; a later stage links them. This applies however short the
  gap and whatever else happened in it.

  REPORTED: "On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed a claim brought in 1999." One sentence.
  ONE ENTRY. The 1999 date belongs in `start_date_span` on the SAME entry as the 2003
  decision, but there is no separate act to quote — leave `institution_narrated_span`
  out.

Do not fold a narrated filing into its decision's entry to keep the count down. A folded
entry can only carry one `quote`, and the quote is supposed to be the passage that
evidences THAT entry — folding leaves one of the two dates quoting nothing. Splitting is
what makes every entry's quote actually support that entry, and it is what a later stage
needs to tell the two acts apart at all.

This is what tells a later stage whether to render the filing and the decision as two
linked acts or as one act carrying both dates; it cannot make that call from
`start_date_span` alone. So do not fill it just because `start_date_span` is present.

THIS FIELD UNDER-FIRES, and that is the single biggest source of missed entries on this
pipeline. Two measured failure modes:

  - Treating a request sentence as scene-setting for the decision that follows it. "On 9
    September 2005 the applicant, through his lawyer, asked the mayor of Rila to bring a
    court action... In a letter of 16 September 2005, the mayor... refused his request" is
    NARRATED, and was left empty anyway.
  - THE FILER DOES NOT HAVE TO BE THE APPLICANT. "In October 1995 the [Port] contacted the
    prosecutor... He requested its release", followed by "On 19 January 1996 the [ship] was
    freed from arrest", is narrated exactly like the worked example -- and was missed
    because the filer was a company. Nothing changes when the filer is a company, an
    official, a relative or a co-accused.

Every "X asked/applied/requested/lodged an appeal" sentence with its own subject and verb
is a candidate, whether or not it is the first such sentence in the document and whether or
not a near-identical request appeared earlier for a different instance. Before moving on,
check each entry against its own source sentences: if the decision's neighbouring sentence
names who asked for it, that naming is the span this field exists for.

### MULTIPLE APPLICANTS BRINGING SEPARATE ACTIONS ARE NEVER ONE ENTRY

Where a judgment has more than one applicant, a sentence narrating their filings or
decisions TOGETHER is not evidence of one shared action -- it is usually shorthand for
reporting two individually-brought actions in one breath. The signal words: "respective
actions", "both decisions", "each of the applicants", "in his submissions the second
applicant indicated". Wherever you see them, give EACH applicant their own entry, even
though the quote, date and outcome you copy are identical for both.

Worked example -- "In October and November 1993 the applicants instituted proceedings
before the Darmstadt Labour Court against ESA... On 21 March 1995 the ... Court declared
the applicants' respective actions inadmissible. In both decisions..." is FOUR entries: a
filing and a decision entry for the first applicant, and the same pair for the second.
"Respective actions" and "both decisions" are the document telling you the court decided
TWICE.

THE PARTY ON EACH SPLIT ENTRY MUST NAME THE ONE APPLICANT IT IS ABOUT -- "the first
applicant" on one, "the second applicant" on the other, copied from wherever the document
distinguishes them. Writing the plural "the applicants" on both is only half the fix: it
shows the court decided twice but throws away which decision was about whom, which is the
whole reason to split. Use "the first applicant" / "the second applicant" even where the
document distinguishes them no further than that.

Where the document gives NO such signal -- a single joint application, one shared outcome,
no "respective"/"each"/"both" language -- one entry naming both applicants is correct. The
split applies only where the text itself marks the actions as separate.

### WHAT ALWAYS STARTS A NEW ENTRY

Under-splitting loses whole appeals. These are NEVER interim spans and ALWAYS get their own
entry:

  - an appeal, a cassation appeal, a constitutional complaint, a case review
  - a remittal, a retrial, a case transferred to another court
  - ANY decision by a body that is not the deciding body of the entry you are writing
  - a prosecutor's ruling, an administrative decision, an enforcement step
  - a detention order or its extension

`interim_spans` is ONLY for steps before the SAME body between its own start date and its
own decision. Putting another court's judgment there deletes that court from the record.

### DETENTION GOES IN ITS OWN FIELD

A decision to detain, release or extend detention is not an interim span. Put it in the
`detention` list of the entry it belongs to, with spans naming who was detained, when it
started and ended, and what the measure was called.

## PARTIES — EVERY ENTRY NEEDS THEM

Somebody brought every step and somebody answered
it. Name the party itself, never their lawyer.

`role_span` must show STANDING IN THIS INSTANCE — who brought it and who answered it — not
the most recent thing they did inside it. Prefer the words showing who lodged, claimed,
applied, appealed, was prosecuted or was sued. A party who files a motion partway through
does NOT thereby become the party who brought the instance: if the applicant sued and the
respondent later moved to strike out, the applicant still brought it. Reading standing off
the last act is the most common way this field goes wrong, and it silently reverses sides.

**THE OTHER SIDE IS OFTEN NAMED ONLY ONCE.** A first-instance judgment names both sides;
the appeal names only whoever appealed, because the other side is already known to the
reader. Where the earlier entry this one continues recorded an opposing party and nothing
suggests they dropped out, record that party here too — otherwise they vanish from the whole upper
half of the chain.

**This is permission, not a quota.** Do not manufacture a second party. Many entries
genuinely have one — an application to an authority, a prosecutor acting of his own motion,
a review opened ex officio — and some have none you can name. ONE PARTY IS A CORRECT
ANSWER. NO PARTIES IS A CORRECT ANSWER. A wrong second party is worse than one right one.

**Never reach for the deciding body OF THIS ENTRY to fill the gap.** The body in this
entry's `authority_span` is not available as this entry's missing party. It is the nearest
name to hand, which is exactly why it is forbidden. If you cannot name the other side,
leave it out.

**But an authority IS the party when its OWN decision is the thing under review** — the
single most-missed party in the corpus. In public-law proceedings the respondent is usually
a public body, and it is a party exactly where it is not the judge:

  - A permit is refused by a District Authority; the applicant appeals to a Regional
    Government, which refuses too; he appeals to an Administrative Court, which QUASHES THE
    REGIONAL GOVERNMENT'S DECISION. On the first two entries those bodies decide. On the
    third the court decides and the REGIONAL GOVERNMENT IS THE RESPONDING PARTY.
  - A ministry rejects a claim and the claimant sues: deciding body on its own decision,
    defendant in the litigation that follows, through every appeal.
  - A prosecutor's refusal is challenged before a court: the prosecutor's office is the
    responding party in that challenge.

The test is WHO IS DECIDING THIS ENTRY. Everyone else named is eligible, including a public
body, including one that decided an earlier entry in the chain. Look at what this entry's
words say was quashed, upheld, appealed against or complained of.

**THE SIDES USUALLY SWAP ON REVIEW, so do not copy `role_span` across.** Whoever lost below
is usually the appellant above. Read who brought THIS instance from THIS entry's words.

## CONTINUATIONS — EVERY STEP IS ITS OWN ENTRY

Do NOT link entries to each other: a
later stage builds the chain from the entries you write, and it can only chain what you
wrote. So the steps that carry a matter forward must each be an entry. Three kinds are
missed constantly:

  a. **The same body acting again in the same matter.** Where an authority takes a
     succession of decisions carrying one process forward -- seizes an asset, values it,
     resolves to sell it, offers it, re-offers it when the sale fails, awards it, transfers
     ownership -- every step is its own entry. A real judgment runs ten such steps: that is
     TEN entries, not one summarising the run.
  b. **A new phase continuing an earlier one.** The investigation that produced the
     charges, the trial, the enforcement of the judgment, the judicial challenge to an
     administrative decision: each is an entry, however different the bodies.
  c. **A remittal going back down.** The fresh hearing after a quashing is a new entry,
     not a second date on the original first-instance entry.

KEEP EVERY STEP, DO NOT MERGE. A claim lodged on 24 February 2011 and dismissed by the 6th
Chamber on 19 March 2014 is TWO entries -- not one entry reading "seeking the annulment"
with the Chamber's date on it. That fusion loses the filing, loses its date, and
misattributes the applicant's own act to the court that ruled on it.

## FINALITY, PENDING AND DETENTION

Copy into `finality_span` any words showing a decision was
final or that remedies were exhausted; into `pending_span` any words showing a matter was
still outstanding when the judgment was written. Where an entry ordered, extended or ended a
deprivation of liberty, record it in that entry's `detention`, with the detainee and the
bounding dates as the text gives them.

## BEFORE YOU FINISH — THE ONE-SENTENCE TEST FOR TWO DATES

Go through your entries once more and stop at EVERY entry that carries BOTH
`start_date_span` and `decision_date_span`. Find the two dates in the document:

  - SAME SENTENCE ("On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed a claim brought in 1999") -> the
    filing is only reported: keep ONE entry.
  - DIFFERENT SENTENCES ("In 2004 the applicant's sister applied to the District Court
    to have him placed under guardianship. In a decision of 3 March 2005 the court
    granted the application.") -> the filing is NARRATED: you folded two acts into one. Rewrite it as TWO
    entries -- the filing (start_date_span, institution_narrated_span = the filing
    sentence, no decision_date_span, no outcome) and then the decision (decision_date_span,
    outcome, no start_date_span).

There is no third case. Two sentences means two entries, every time, whoever filed --
the applicant, a relative, a lawyer, a prosecutor, a company -- and however short the
text between them. A request to a mayor, a council or a welfare office and its refusal are
a filing and a decision too.

Splitting changes the NUMBER of entries and nothing else. Both new entries keep the
original entry's `instance_level`, `proceeding_type`, authority and track: a request to a
municipal council and the council's answer are both administrative review, a prosecutor's
refusal is still investigative. Never re-classify an entry because you split it.

## BEFORE YOU FINISH — COVERAGE CHECK

List every body that decided anything about these applicants. The list is wider than courts:

  - courts, tribunals, appeal benches, arbitration bodies
  - prosecutors at every level
  - municipal councils, ministries, mayors, governors
  - **social assistance and welfare departments** — granting, refusing, increasing or
    reducing an allowance is a domestic event with a decision and a date
  - **police and enforcement officers** — a search for a person, an arrest, a return to an
    institution, a seizure, a payment order carried out
  - **the end of a detention** — "the applicant was released from detention" is an event
    with a date even where no authority is named; record it with `authority_kind_span`
    omitted rather than dropping it for lack of a named decider
  - **medical, psychiatric and social assessment bodies** whose findings decided something
  - care homes, hospitals, guardianship authorities, registrars

EVERY ONE of them must be the `authority_span` of at least one entry.

The non-judicial periphery is what gets missed: a welfare decision or a police action reads
as background narrative rather than as a step. Measured on one judgment, ALL of these were
absent though each is dated and each has an acting body — a 1990 unfitness-to-work
declaration by occupational physicians, a welfare report noting a psychiatric diagnosis,
the applicant's transport to a care home, a police search after he went missing and his
return, and an increase in his monthly allowance. None had been folded into a neighbouring
entry either; they were simply not read as procedural steps. Read every paragraph asking
"did a named body do something to or for this applicant on a date", not only "did a court
decide something".

If a body appears in your reading but not in your entries, you have merged away a real step
— add it. If the same body decided twice on different matters or different dates, that is
two entries, not one.

HEDGED LANGUAGE IS NOT A REASON TO DROP AN ENTRY. "It appears that the order was lifted in
May 1996" is the document's own uncertainty about a fact it still asserts happened — copy
"it appears that" into the span along with the rest. The same goes for "apparently", "it
seems" and "according to the applicant".

JUDGES ARE NOT PERSONS HERE. Never give a judge, magistrate, presiding officer, registrar or
rapporteur -- of a domestic court or of this Court -- an entry in `persons` or in any
`parties` list. Judicial office-holders are recorded elsewhere, deterministically; the
deciding BODY belongs in `authority_span` and nowhere else.

Otherwise, where the document names a person -- applicant, lawyer, guardian, relative,
witness, co-accused -- give them an entry in `persons`, and copy a gender cue if the document marks
them. Cues are easy to miss: a person introduced as initials and called "she" twenty
paragraphs later still has one.

Be exhaustive about evidence and silent about judgement. If the document does not say, do
not supply it.
