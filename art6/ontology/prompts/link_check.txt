TASK: You are given the domestic events extracted from a European Court of Human Rights
judgment, the links a planner drew between them, and the judgment itself. CHECK EVERY
LINK. You do not add, remove, merge or reclassify events, and you do not change tracks.

A link says: this event's IMMEDIATE PROCEDURAL PREDECESSOR is that event -- the one it
appeals, reviews, enforces, answers, challenges or was brought because of. "Immediate" is
procedural, not chronological. For each event that has a link, read the two events'
quotes in the judgment and decide:

  keep   the predecessor is right.
  move   the event continues something, but a DIFFERENT candidate is its immediate
         predecessor. Name it.
  drop   nothing in the candidates is a procedural antecedent; the event heads a chain.

And for an event with NO link that plainly answers an earlier one:

  add    name the predecessor.

THE MISTAKES TO LOOK FOR -- each is common:

  - SKIPPING A STEP. A cassation ruling linked to the first-instance judgment when an
    appeal ruling sits between them; a second appeal linked to the original decision
    rather than the ruling it actually challenges. Move it to the step in between.
  - LINKING TO A STEP INSIDE A PROCEEDING. A hearing, an interview, a questioning by the
    investigating judge, an expert's report is not what the next proceeding answers;
    the decision (or the filing of that proceeding) is. Move it there.
  - THE WRONG ONE OF SEVERAL SIMILAR EVENTS. Repeated requests, extensions or refusals by
    the same body: each continues the one just before it, or the specific decision its
    own sentence names ("appealed against the decision of 12 May"). Check the dates.
  - LINKS BY PROXIMITY. Two events next to each other in time, or before the same court,
    with no procedural relation. Drop, or move to what the event did respond to.
  - A FILING AND ITS DECISION. A decision continues the filing it disposes of; the next
    proceeding (an appeal) continues that decision.

Use the judgment's own words: "appealed against that judgment", "upheld it", "following
the Supreme Court's ruling", "in response to his request of 3 May". Where they name the
decision being challenged, that decision is the predecessor.

The predecessor you name MUST be one of the event's `candidates`. Do not touch a link you
have no reason to doubt -- say keep.

## OUTPUT

A single JSON object, no prose around it. One verdict for EVERY linked event, plus any
`add`:

{
  "verdicts": [
    {"event": "e7", "verdict": "keep"},
    {"event": "e9", "verdict": "move", "predecessor": "e8", "kind": "continues",
     "reason": "the appeal is against the Regional Court's ruling of 4 May, not the first
       judgment", "evidence_span": "verbatim words from the judgment"},
    {"event": "e12", "verdict": "drop", "reason": "..."},
    {"event": "e14", "verdict": "add", "predecessor": "e13", "kind": "continues",
     "evidence_span": "..."}
  ]
}

`kind` is "continues" or "during", as in the links you are checking.
