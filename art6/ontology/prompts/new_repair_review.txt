  `events_chained_to_nothing` is the most important of these, and the one
  most often left unfixed. Each names an event that continues nothing and is
  continued by nothing -- an island in what is supposed to be a procedural
  history. For each, read the document and ask what came before it in the SAME
  matter, then add `echr:followsProceeding` pointing at that event.

  What counts as continuing is wider than an appeal, and appeals are the only
  kind anyone gets right. A body acting again in the same matter continues its
  own earlier act: an authority that seizes an asset, resolves to sell it,
  re-offers it when the sale fails, then transfers ownership has made four
  linked decisions, not four unrelated ones. A new phase continues the earlier
  phase: a trial follows the investigation that produced the charges, an
  enforcement follows the judgment it enforces, a judicial challenge follows
  the administrative decision it contests -- the bodies being different is not
  a reason to break the chain, it is usually where the chain matters most. A
  fresh hearing after a remittal follows the quashing, not the original
  decision.

  LINK, DO NOT MERGE. Two events joined by echr:followsProceeding stay two
  events, each keeping its own court, date and quote. Never fold one into the
  other.

  Some islands are correct: a genuinely separate track has a head that follows
  nothing, and a one-off administrative act may stand alone. Leave those.

  THE TEST IS "THE SAME MATTER CONTINUED", NOT "THE NEXT THING THAT HAPPENED".
  Two events chain only if the later one carries forward the SAME dispute,
  charge or claim, about the SAME people. Nearness in time is not continuity,
  and neither is sharing a court, a prosecutor or an investigator.

  Three things are NOT continuations, and each is a track of its own that
  correctly follows nothing:

    - PROCEEDINGS AGAINST A DIFFERENT PERSON. Where a co-accused, a witness or
      a third party is tried and convicted in their own proceedings, that is
      their track, not the applicant's. Two convictions weeks apart before the
      same court are still two separate matters if they convict two different
      people. Chaining them asserts a procedural continuity that does not
      exist.
    - A NEW MATTER ARISING LATER. A fresh investigation opened into new
      allegations after the original case ended does not continue that case
      merely because it involves the same applicant. Ask what it is ABOUT: new
      subject matter is a new track, however suggestive the timing.
    - A PARALLEL TRACK RUNNING ALONGSIDE. Civil and criminal proceedings over
      the same facts run beside each other and neither continues the other.

  When you are unsure whether two events are one matter or two, LEAVE THEM
  UNLINKED. A missing link is a gap a reader can see. A wrong link is a
  procedural history that reads as authoritative and is false, and nothing
  downstream can tell it from a true one.
