This is the DOCUMENT-LED FIX pass. You are given the finished graph AND the full text of the judgment the case was decided on. Every earlier pass saw only the graph; you are the one pass here that can see what the extraction MISSED, and can therefore fix a defect that can only be answered from the document itself.

The graph may have been built from a CONDENSED version of that judgment rather than the judgment itself, so do not assume everything below is already represented, or that anything absent from the graph was judged and excluded. A step the condensed version dropped never reached the pass that built the graph at all, and looks exactly like a step that was never in the document. The document above is the authority; the graph is a claim about it.

The EXTRACTION INSTRUCTIONS reproduced above bind you, not just the pass that ran under them. They are not background reading: check the graph against them, not only against the two jobs below -- the second job names the clearest recurring case of this, but it is not the only one.

THIS PASS DOES NOT MINT NEW EVENTS. Finding a whole domestic proceeding the graph has no node for at all is a SEPARATE pass's job (global recall), run on its own with its own guidance for judging a new event's rung, outcome and matter from raw prose. Confine yourself here to the graph's EXISTING nodes: fixing their chaining, their participation, their anchors, and the shape of a node the instructions forbid. If, while reading the document, you notice a whole proceeding with no node at all, say so in your rationale and leave it -- do not mint it here.

YOU HAVE TWO JOBS.

FIRST, the GAPS listed above: events chained to nothing, events with no parties recorded, proceedings with no deciding authority, persons linked to nothing. Earlier passes were shown none of these, on purpose. Filling a gap means knowing who the parties were or which court it was, and that is written in the document -- so a pass that cannot read the document can only guess, and when it guessed it reached for whatever node was nearest, naming courts as parties to their own cases. You can read the document, so these are yours.

  For each gap: find the passage that answers it and add what it supports,
  with a verbatim echr:hasSupportingQuote for any new party you name. A
  participation you add needs exactly one echr:participatingParty (the party
  itself -- never their lawyer, guardian or representative, and NEVER the
  body that decided the event) and one echr:hasPartySide.

  `events_chained_to_nothing` is the most important of these, and the one
  most often left unfixed. Each names an event that continues nothing and is
  continued by nothing -- an island in what is supposed to be a procedural
  history. For each, read the document and ask what came before it in the SAME
  matter, then add `echr:followsProceeding` pointing at that event.

  What counts as continuing is wider than an appeal, and appeals are the only
  kind anyone gets right. A body acting again in the same matter continues its
  own earlier act: an authority that seizes an asset, resolves to sell it,
  re-offers it when the sale fails, then transfers ownership has made four
  linked decisions, not four unrelated ones. A new phase continues the earlier
  phase: a trial follows the investigation that produced the charges, an
  enforcement follows the judgment it enforces, a judicial challenge follows
  the administrative decision it contests -- the bodies being different is not
  a reason to break the chain, it is usually where the chain matters most. A
  fresh hearing after a remittal follows the quashing, not the original
  decision.

  LINK, DO NOT MERGE. Two events joined by echr:followsProceeding stay two
  events, each keeping its own court, date and quote. Never fold one into the
  other.

  Some islands are correct: a genuinely separate track has a head that follows
  nothing, and a one-off administrative act may stand alone. Leave those.

  THE TEST IS "THE SAME MATTER CONTINUED", NOT "THE NEXT THING THAT HAPPENED".
  Two events chain only if the later one carries forward the SAME dispute,
  charge or claim, about the SAME people. Nearness in time is not continuity,
  and neither is sharing a court, a prosecutor or an investigator.

  Three things are NOT continuations, and each is a track of its own that
  correctly follows nothing:

    - PROCEEDINGS AGAINST A DIFFERENT PERSON. Where a co-accused, a witness or
      a third party is tried and convicted in their own proceedings, that is
      their track, not the applicant's. Two convictions weeks apart before the
      same court are still two separate matters if they convict two different
      people. Chaining them asserts a procedural continuity that does not
      exist.
    - A NEW MATTER ARISING LATER. A fresh investigation opened into new
      allegations after the original case ended does not continue that case
      merely because it involves the same applicant. Ask what it is ABOUT: new
      subject matter is a new track, however suggestive the timing.
    - A PARALLEL TRACK RUNNING ALONGSIDE. Civil and criminal proceedings over
      the same facts run beside each other and neither continues the other.

  When you are unsure whether two events are one matter or two, LEAVE THEM
  UNLINKED. A missing link is a gap a reader can see. A wrong link is a
  procedural history that reads as authoritative and is false, and nothing
  downstream can tell it from a true one.

  `events_missing_participation` names events with no parties recorded. A
  participation you add needs a verbatim echr:hasSupportingQuote showing THIS
  party taking part in THIS event -- not the event's own quote. Without one it
  will be refused outright, whole, and the event left as it was. That is the
  intended outcome, not a failure: recording a party the document does not
  place there is worse than recording none, because nothing downstream can
  tell an invented party from a read one. Participation is not required.

  `quotes_not_found_in_source` names anchors whose text is not in the document.
  Earlier stages cannot touch these at all -- not to fix, not even to move --
  so they reach you untouched and you are the only stage that can act. For
  each, find the passage that actually supports the claim and replace the span
  with it, copied character for character. Where the document supports the
  claim nowhere, remove the anchor and say so in the rationale: a node left
  honestly unevidenced beats one carrying a quote that does not check out.
  Never "correct" a quote by tidying it into something that merely reads well.

  You will also see nodes with no anchor that an earlier stage created by
  splitting a conflated event in two. Those are not errors -- the split was
  right, and that stage is forbidden from moving the original quote onto the
  new node. Anchor them from the document.

  WHERE THE DOCUMENT DOES NOT SAY, LEAVE THE GAP. This is the whole point of
  routing these to you. An event recorded with no parties is honest and can
  be fixed later by a human or a better extraction; an event recorded with
  parties the document never names is a false statement in a released
  dataset, and nothing downstream can tell it apart from a true one. If you
  cannot point at the words, do not write the triple. Returning a gap
  unanswered is a correct answer, not a failure.

You may also correct a fact the document plainly contradicts -- a wrong date, a decision attributed to the wrong court -- on an event ALREADY IN THE GRAPH. Say in the rationale what the text says. This includes a chronological-order violation flagged by the validator (a decision date earlier than its own start date, a resumption before its adjournment, or a procedural chain running backwards): earlier passes cannot see the document and so left it alone -- you can, so check the text and fix whichever date is actually wrong.

Give any date you write the datatype matching the precision the text gives -- xsd:date for a full date, xsd:gYearMonth for a year and month, xsd:gYear for a bare year -- never a plain string.

SECOND, check that no node states a fact the EXTRACTION INSTRUCTIONS forbid, in particular the person/party rules: ONE echr:NaturalPerson per human being, never one node for a group. The clearest tell is a case whose name joins two or more people ("X and Y v. State") or whose text gives several applicants distinguishing details -- separate birth years, separate residences, "the first applicant" / "the second applicant" -- while the graph's echr:hasApplicant links resolve to only one echr:NaturalPerson, or one node carries more than one value for a property that describes a single person (echr:hasPersonName, echr:hasBirthYear, rdfs:label, echr:hasGenderCueText). That node is two people folded into one, and an earlier pass may already have pruned it down to a single, single-valued node that now looks correctly formed -- do not take "the graph validates" as evidence it is right; re-derive the applicant count from the case name and the document's own description of them, not from what currently has a node.

  Where you confirm a false merge: mint the missing echr:NaturalPerson(s), typed echr:Party too, each carrying only the values the document gives THAT person -- their own echr:hasPersonName, echr:hasBirthYear, echr:hasNationality, echr:hasPlaceOfResidence, echr:hasGender with its echr:hasGenderCue and echr:hasGenderCueText -- and a echr:hasSupportingQuote copied verbatim from the document (the sentence describing them jointly, e.g. "born in 1959 and 1971 respectively", verbatim-supports each individually and may be reused for both). Add the new node to the case document's echr:hasApplicant. This is a PERSON node, not a domestic event, and is not subject to the "no new events here" rule above.

  Then re-point every echr:Participation and every echr:PreTrialDetention's echr:hasDetainee link that the merged node was carrying: read each one's own echr:hasSupportingQuote (and, where that alone does not name a person, the quote of the event it belongs to) and decide which of the now-separate people it actually names -- an ordinal ("the second applicant"), a name, or a pronoun tied to the nearest antecedent. Point echr:participatingParty / echr:hasDetainee at that person alone. LINK, DO NOT DUPLICATE: each participation still names exactly one party. Where a participation's own evidence does not distinguish which person it was, leave it on whichever node the surrounding chain already ties it to and say so in the rationale -- do not guess, and do not strip it of a party to avoid guessing.

  This check is not limited to persons -- any node the instructions say must not exist in the form it takes (a company typed echr:NaturalPerson, a court named as its own case's echr:participatingParty) gets the same treatment: fix it if the document settles it, leave it and say why in the rationale if it does not.

BE CONSERVATIVE. Fix what the document settles and no more. Do not add an event because a case like this usually has one; do not infer a step from a gap in the dates; do not model the Strasbourg proceedings themselves, or other cases cited as precedent. If the graph already covers the document, return an empty patch -- that is the correct answer and a common one.
