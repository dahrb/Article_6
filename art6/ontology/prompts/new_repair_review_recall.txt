This is the GLOBAL RECALL pass. You are given the finished graph AND the full text of the judgment the case was decided on. Your one job is the thing no earlier pass can do at all: find domestic events that HAPPENED, before the case reached Strasbourg, and have NO NODE in the graph -- and add them.

The graph may have been built from a CONDENSED version of that judgment rather than the judgment itself, so do not assume everything below is already represented, or that anything absent from the graph was judged and excluded. A step the condensed version dropped never reached the pass that built the graph at all, and looks exactly like a step that was never in the document. The document above is the authority; the graph is a claim about it.

YOUR JOB: read the case text and compare it, paragraph by paragraph, against the graph. A hearing, a decision, an appeal, an administrative refusal, an enforcement step, a prosecutor's ruling -- anything before the case reached Strasbourg that affected the applicant and has no node -- is yours to add. This is the only pass permitted to add a domestic event, and the only one permitted to add an evidence anchor, because it is the only one reading the document to find the fact.

A DATE IS OFTEN THE BEST WAY TO FIND A CANDIDATE. Scanning for every sentence that names a body acting ON A DATE is a more reliable way to surface a missing event than trying to recognise "a domestic proceeding" in the abstract -- a dated sentence naming who did what is exactly the shape of the events already in the graph, and the ones the graph is missing look the same. Treat every dated sentence you have not matched to an existing node as a live candidate worth checking. That said, a date alone does not make something an event: having found the candidate, still check it actually fits the schema below before minting it -- a date attached to background narration, a fact about the world, or a step this schema explicitly excludes (see WHAT IS NOT AN EVENT OF ITS OWN below) stays out even though it is dated.

BEFORE YOU MINT A CANDIDATE, CHECK IT AGAINST THE GRAPH'S EXISTING NODES, not only against the other candidates you are considering. The graph may already carry the act you are looking at, under a paraphrase, a different label, or folded into a neighbouring node's own quote -- read every existing echr:DomesticProceeding, echr:AdministrativeAction, echr:EnforcementAction and echr:ProsecutorialReview that shares a rough date or authority with your candidate, and read ITS OWN echr:hasSupportingQuote before deciding the act has no node. A "second" or "further" application, refusal or ruling is genuinely a distinct act only where the text gives it distinct content of its own (a different applicant, a different subject, a materially different date) -- where it turns out to be the SAME act the existing node already anchors, restated or referred back to elsewhere in the document, that is not a gap, and minting a second node for it creates the exact duplicate this pass exists to avoid, not fill.

EVERY EVENT YOU ADD MUST CARRY ALL THREE OF THESE, in the same group that mints it, or the WHOLE GROUP IS DISCARDED and the event does not land:

  1. echr:hasSupportingQuote, copied character for character from the text
     above. Not paraphrased, not tidied, not stitched together from two
     passages. An unevidenced addition is indistinguishable from an
     invention.
  2. echr:hasCourt naming the authority that decided it.
  3. echr:hasParticipation pointing at a participation node that YOU also
     create in the same group, carrying both echr:participatingParty (the
     party itself, never their lawyer or guardian) and echr:hasPartySide.
     A bare link to an empty participation node does not count.

A half-specified event is not a partial success -- it is a new defect, and you
are better off not adding it at all. If the document does not tell you who
decided the event or who took part in it, DO NOT ADD THE EVENT.

Give each new event its class (echr:DomesticProceeding for a step before a court, echr:AdministrativeAction for a non-judicial decision by a state body, echr:EnforcementAction for carrying out an earlier decision, echr:ProsecutorialReview for a decision inside a prosecutor's office), its date, its echr:hasInstanceLevel, its echr:hasOutcome and echr:hasOutcomeDirection where the text shows something was decided, its echr:hasMatter where the event is substantively about one of the values that vocabulary names, and a echr:followsProceeding link to whatever it continues or reviews. See HOW TO CLASSIFY A NEW EVENT below for how to decide each of these from the text, and the EXTRACTION INSTRUCTIONS above for the ontology term each classification maps onto.

You may also correct a fact the document plainly contradicts on an event already in the graph -- a wrong date, a decision attributed to the wrong court. Say in the rationale what the text says. This includes a chronological-order violation flagged by the validator (a decision date earlier than its own start date, a resumption before its adjournment, or a procedural chain running backwards): earlier passes cannot see the document and so left it alone -- you can, so check the text and fix whichever date is actually wrong.

Give any date you write the datatype matching the precision the text gives -- xsd:date for a full date, xsd:gYearMonth for a year and month, xsd:gYear for a bare year -- never a plain string.

THIS PASS DOES NOT FIX EXISTING NODES. Chaining an event that is already in the graph, filling a participation it is missing, replacing an anchor that does not check out, or splitting a person wrongly merged with another -- all of that belongs to a SEPARATE pass, run on its own. Confine yourself here to events that have NO NODE AT ALL. If, while reading the document, you notice an existing node with a wrong link or a missing party, say so in your rationale and leave it for that pass rather than touching it here.

## HOW TO CLASSIFY A NEW EVENT

Every event you add needs a rung (echr:hasInstanceLevel) and, where something was decided, a result (echr:hasOutcome / echr:hasOutcomeDirection). These are judgements you make from what the body DID, not phrases you wait to find written down.

RUNG -- read what the body did:

  - a first hearing of a claim, charge or application   -> first instance
  - a review of a lower body's decision on the merits    -> appeal
  - a review limited to points of law                    -> cassation
  - a step inside a criminal investigation (a warrant,
    an arrest, a prosecutor's refusal, an expert order)   -> investigative
  - an internal review by an administrative body          -> administrative review
  - a review of an already-decided stage by a body
    external to the panel/instance that decided it,
    outside the ordinary appeal ladder                    -> case review
  - a case reopened after a final decision                -> reopening

RESULT -- read what happened to the substance:

  - the body ruled on the substance, whichever way       -> merits decided
  - a claim or appeal refused without reaching merits     -> inadmissible
  - sent back to a lower body                             -> remitted
  - withdrawn, discontinued or settled                    -> settled or withdrawn
  - a real outcome none of these fit                      -> other

These five are the WHOLE vocabulary. There is no value for "an order to do
something": a body that grants a request -- appointing a guardian, allowing an
injunction, ordering a release -- has ruled on the substance of that request,
so it is merits decided.

AN ACT IS NOT A RESULT. Leave the outcome OUT when nothing was decided -- the
rung is unaffected, classify it as always. A gun seized, a search warrant
issued, a suspect arrested, a crime scene inspected: these are things DONE,
not requests answered, and none of the five outcome values is true of them.
The test is whether something was ASKED and ANSWERED. A request refused
during an investigation IS a result (inadmissible); an arrest answers
nothing, and does not.

MATTER, where the event is substantively about one of: arrest, detention
(placing/continuing/ending pre-trial detention -- contrast bail), bail
(release pending trial), charge, conviction (a verdict or an appeal against
one), investigation (a search, seizure, expert order, a request refused
during an investigation), damages, custody (custody of a person, or legal
capacity/guardianship of an adult), employment, pension, property,
extradition, legal aid, vetting, disciplinary (a professional licence
suspended, revoked or restored). Most events are about none of these and get
no `matter` at all -- do not default to `other` or `unknown` for an event
with no identifiable substantive matter (a costs order, a purely
housekeeping ruling).

WHAT IS NOT AN EVENT OF ITS OWN: trial management. An ordinary ruling on the
conduct of a hearing already in progress -- admitting or excluding evidence,
refusing to sever counts, ruling there is a case to answer, refusing to
extend time for a submission -- is part of how the trial reached its verdict,
not a step in the procedural history. Do not add it, even where it was asked
and refused: being refused does not turn trial management into an event. The
exception is a ruling that authorises or ends a deprivation of liberty (bail
granted, withdrawn, detention ordered or extended -- always add these), alters
a right independent of the trial's merits (refusing chosen defence counsel,
refusing to disqualify the judge for bias, refusing legal aid), or itself
disposes of the case or a charge in it (a directed acquittal, a severance
removing a defendant or count). A case-disposing ruling still needs its OWN
date from its OWN sentence to be its own node -- never borrow the trial's
start or end date from a different sentence to manufacture one for it; if the
text gives it no date of its own, it is not a separate event.

ONE NEW NODE PER JURISDICTIONAL INSTANCE, not per procedural act. A body's own
filing and its own decision on that filing are one node, not two -- unless the
document narrates the filing as its OWN act in its OWN sentence, separate from
the decision ("the applicant instituted proceedings... the court dismissed the
claim"), in which case mint the filing and the decision as separate nodes
joined by echr:partOfProceeding. A DIFFERENT body taking the matter (an
appeal, a transfer, a remittal) always starts a genuinely new node.

TWO PROCEEDINGS SHARING A COURT AND A DATE ARE OFTEN NOT THE SAME PROCEEDING.
Where you are deciding whether a passage describes ONE missing event or TWO,
do not collapse them onto one node merely because they share an authority and
a decision date -- a court deciding two genuinely different things on one day
is unusual but not rare, and folding them into one node loses a proceeding the
document actually states. Treat any of these as evidence they are TWO events,
not one reported twice:

  - the parties differ (one side's own appeal against a judgment, and the
    OTHER side's appeal against a different judgment, decided by the same
    court the same day)
  - the quotes describe different acts even where they share a sentence
    ("both requests were rejected" can dispose of two different applications,
    filed by different people about different things, in the one sentence
    that gives you the date)
  - one quote is explicitly marked as first-of-two or second-of-two in the
    source ("a second judgment of the same date", "based on the same
    reasoning as its first judgment")

Only where the parties match (or neither carries any) and the quotes read as
two reports of the identical act -- not two acts that happen to read alike --
is a court-plus-date collision actually one event. When genuinely unsure,
mint both as separate nodes: a wrongly-split pair is a defect a later pass can
still merge, a wrongly-collapsed pair reads as one correct proceeding and the
lost one is gone for good.

MULTIPLE APPLICANTS ARE NEVER ONE EVENT. Where a sentence disposes of several
applicants' filings together -- "the applicants' respective actions were
declared inadmissible", "both requests were rejected" -- that is the document
reporting several individually-brought actions in one breath, not one joint
action. Add ONE new node PER APPLICANT even though the quote, date and
outcome you copy for each is identical, and name which applicant each is
about ("the first applicant" / "the second applicant") rather than the
undifferentiated plural.

THE NON-JUDICIAL PERIPHERY IS WHERE MOST MISSED EVENTS LIVE. Before you finish,
check specifically for: social assistance and welfare departments (granting,
refusing, increasing or reducing an allowance); police and enforcement
officers (a search for a person, an arrest, a return to an institution, a
seizure, a payment order carried out); the end of a detention ("the applicant
was released" is an event with a date even where no authority is named for
it -- add it with echr:hasCourt omitted rather than dropping it); medical,
psychiatric and social assessment bodies whose findings decided something;
care homes, hospitals, guardianship authorities, registrars. Each of these is
easy to read as background narrative rather than as a step with a
decision-maker and a date -- it is a step, and it needs a node.

HEDGED LANGUAGE IS NOT A REASON TO LEAVE AN EVENT OUT. "It appears that the
order was lifted in May 1996" is the document's own uncertainty about a fact
it is still asserting happened -- add the event, with the hedge folded into
its quote, rather than treating the hedge as a reason to omit it.

BE CONSERVATIVE. Add what the document states and no more. Do not add an
event because a case like this usually has one; do not infer a step from a
gap in the dates; do not model the Strasbourg proceedings themselves, or
other cases cited as precedent. If the graph already covers the document,
return an empty patch -- that is the correct answer and a common one.
