This call checks echr:hasSupportingQuote fidelity. Unlike every other stage, you are given the document's FULL SOURCE TEXT below, in the <<<SOURCE block, and `unverified_quotes` lists every supporting quote in the graph that does NOT appear in it.

A supporting quote is an evidence anchor: it must be a VERBATIM span of the source document, so that any fact it supports can be traced back and checked by a reader. A quote that is paraphrased, reworded, stitched together from separate passages, or invented outright is not evidence -- it is an assertion dressed as one, and it is worse than having no quote at all, because it defeats the check.

Each entry gives you an `index`, the `subject` the quote hangs off, that subject's `subject_types` and `subject_label`, and the exact `quote` text. Work through every entry:

1. FIND THE REAL SPAN. Read the source text in the <<<SOURCE block and look for the passage the quote was clearly meant to capture. Extraction usually paraphrases something real rather than fabricating from nothing -- a quote that mangles wording, modernises spelling, drops an interpolation, or merges two adjacent sentences still points at a genuine passage. If you find it, `remove` the bad quote and `add` echr:hasSupportingQuote on the SAME subject with the corrected text copied EXACTLY from the source, character for character, including its punctuation and capitalisation. Do not tidy it.

2. IF THERE IS NO SUCH SPAN, REMOVE THE QUOTE. When the source genuinely does not say this -- the quote describes something the document never states, or refers to a different case entirely -- `remove` the triple and add nothing. The subject keeps its other properties; it simply loses an anchor it never had. Do NOT invent a replacement quote to fill the gap, and do NOT substitute a loosely related passage just to leave the subject with something. An unanchored fact is recoverable; a falsely anchored one is not.

3. NEVER TOUCH THE SUBJECT'S OTHER PROPERTIES. This stage repairs evidence anchors only. Dates, outcomes, courts, names and links are other stages' business, even when the quote you are fixing makes one of them look wrong.

NEVER RETYPE A QUOTE YOU ARE REMOVING. On every `remove` of echr:hasSupportingQuote, set `quote_index` to the `index` of the entry you are acting on and leave `object` empty. The pipeline looks the literal up by index and removes that one value and no other. This is exact even when the subject holds several quotes and only one is bad, and it costs you two tokens instead of a hundred.

Retyping is how this stage used to fail. A quote is around a hundred characters; reproduced from memory it comes back subtly reworded, matches no triple in the graph, and the remove is discarded as "triple not present" -- while the `add` half of the pair lands regardless, leaving the subject with BOTH the original bad anchor and a new one. Use the index and that cannot happen.

The `add` half is the one place you must still type text out, and it must be copied from the <<<SOURCE block exactly. An add whose text does not appear verbatim in the source is rejected outright, so there is nothing to gain by approximating.

Emit one RepairGroup per subject you touch, with the rationale naming what you found in the source (or that you searched and it is not there).
