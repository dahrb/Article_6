TASK: Read this European Court of Human Rights judgment and SELECT THE EVIDENCE for the
domestic procedural history — everything that happened in the respondent State before the
case reached Strasbourg.

You are a SELECTOR, not a summariser. Every value you output must be text COPIED CHARACTER
FOR CHARACTER from the judgment — not paraphrased, not tidied, not stitched together from
two places. If you cannot copy it exactly, leave the field out. Keep each span SHORT — the
few words that carry the fact.

The rules below are the annotation guide used to build this corpus by hand, condensed for a
single-pass reading. Read it once, then work from the OUTPUT shape at the end, which is
where every field below actually lands.

================================================================================
1. SCOPE
================================================================================

IN SCOPE: the domestic (national) procedural history, wherever the judgment narrates it —
normally "THE FACTS" / "THE CIRCUMSTANCES OF THE CASE", and in decisions also "PROCEDURE".

OUT OF SCOPE, always: proceedings before the European Court of Human Rights itself (the
domestic chain ends at the last national decision); "RELEVANT DOMESTIC LAW AND PRACTICE"
and any other case cited as legal authority or comparison rather than as a step in this
applicant's own history; the Court's own legal reasoning, admissibility analysis and
Article 41 award.

Scope is the WHOLE narrated history, not just the exhaustion chain. All of these are in:
PRIOR proceedings (an earlier conviction, an administrative decision later challenged —
the challenge follows it); PARALLEL proceedings (a civil claim running alongside a
criminal case — independent chains are normal, each with its own head); CONSEQUENT
proceedings (enforcement, reopening, supervisory review after the chain otherwise closed);
proceedings where the applicant was not a party but which the facts narrate as part of
their history (a guardian's application concerning them, a prosecutor's decision about a
relative's death).

================================================================================
2. THE UNIT — ONE EVENT OR TWO?
================================================================================

The unit is ONE AUTHORITY DECIDING ONCE. Consequences:

  - A hearing and the judgment closing it on the same date are ONE event. Several hearings
    across two years before one court, ending in one judgment, are still ONE event: put the
    institution date in start_date_span and the judgment date in decision_date_span.
  - A first-instance trial and its appeal are TWO events, linked by follows.
  - Remittal makes a new event even before the same court — a retrial after remittal is a
    separate proceeding linked by follows, not folded into the original.
  - An administrative decision and the court challenge to it are TWO events, the challenge
    linked back by follows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 A FILING NARRATED SEPARATELY FROM ITS DECISION IS TWO EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where the text separately narrates the institution of a proceeding and the decision closing
it, write TWO entries:

  THE FILING    start_date_span, institution_narrated_span (the filing sentence), NO
                decision_date_span, NO outcome. follows = whatever it responded to, or
                nothing if it heads a chain.
  THE DECISION  decision_date_span, the outcome, NO start_date_span. follows = the filing's
                id (the filing is the id that stands for the whole proceeding going
                forward — anything that later reviews, appeals or enforces this proceeding
                follows the filing, not the decision).

APPLY THIS WHENEVER THE TEXT NARRATES BOTH ACTS, however short the interval and whatever
else happened in between. The question is only whether the judgment gives two sentences or
one, not how long the proceeding took:

  NARRATED (two sentences, two acts -> TWO entries): "On 15 March 1999 the applicant
  instituted civil proceedings against his employer... On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed
  the claim."

  REPORTED (one sentence -> ONE entry): "On 5 June 2003 the court dismissed a claim brought
  in 1999." The institution is reported, not narrated — there is no second act to quote.
  Put 1999 in start_date_span and 2003 in decision_date_span on the SAME entry, and leave
  institution_narrated_span out.

  A request and the decision refusing it, both narrated, are TWO events. Three refused
  requests, each narrated twice, are six.

THE REASON IS THE QUOTE: each entry's span must be the passage that evidences THAT entry. A
folded entry has two distinct textual acts and can carry only one, so one of its two dates
is always left unsupported. Splitting is what makes every entry's span actually support
that entry.

THE FILER DOES NOT HAVE TO BE THE APPLICANT. A company, an official, a relative or a
co-accused asking for something is narrated exactly the same way — "In October 1995 the
Port contacted the prosecutor... He requested its release", followed later by "the ship was
freed from arrest", is NARRATED, two entries, even though the filer is a company.

AN UNDATED FILING DOES NOT GET ITS OWN ENTRY. "The prosecutor appealed", with no date, is
narrated but undatable — keep it inside the decision's entry rather than writing a second
entry with no date to place it.

WHEN GENUINELY AMBIGUOUS whether something is one event or two, write one event. This is
not a rule to reach for on suspicion: either the text narrates the filing as its own act,
with a sentence you can quote, or it does not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 A SHARED DISPOSAL SENTENCE DOES NOT MEAN A SHARED ENTRY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Both requests were rejected by the Regional Court", answering two requests about
different subjects, is TWO entries even though one sentence disposes of both and is the
only date either gets. Ask what each request was ABOUT, not how the ruling is reported: a
motion to disqualify the judge for bias and a motion to admit a different lawyer are
separate matters on their own facts, sharing a date and an outcome span across two entries
— that is correct here, not duplication to avoid. Worked example — "the applicant's civil
defender requested the Regional Court to dismiss the judge presiding in the case for
bias... On the same day the applicant requested to admit Mrs Moskalenko as his lawyer...
Both requests were rejected": entry A is the bias motion (institution_narrated_span
"requested ... to dismiss the judge ... for bias", decision_date_span the shared date,
outcome_span "Both requests were rejected", party the civil defender); entry B is the
lawyer motion (institution_narrated_span "requested to admit Mrs Moskalenko as his
lawyer", the SAME decision_date_span and outcome_span, party the applicant). One entry
with both requests folded into its parties list is the wrong shape — it would have to fit
a single outcome that cannot truthfully describe two different matters at once. The same
applies to a court's further rulings issued alongside a judgment the same day: further
orders on the conduct of an investigation, issued the same day as a conviction, are a
separate entry from the conviction even though only the date links them.

Where a judgment has MORE THAN ONE APPLICANT, a sentence narrating their filings or
decisions together ("respective actions", "both decisions", "each of the applicants") is
usually shorthand for reporting separately-brought actions in one breath — give EACH
applicant their own entry, even where the quote, date and outcome are identical for both.
Worked example — "In October and November 1993 the applicants instituted proceedings
before the Darmstadt Labour Court against ESA... On 21 March 1995 the Court declared the
applicants' respective actions inadmissible. In both decisions..." is FOUR entries: a
filing and a decision entry for the first applicant, and the same pair for the second.
"Respective actions" and "both decisions" are the document telling you the court decided
TWICE. Name which applicant each split entry is about — "the first applicant" on one, "the
second applicant" on the other — rather than writing the plural "the applicants" on both:
that shows the court decided twice but throws away which decision was about whom, which is
the whole reason to split. Where the document gives no such signal — one joint
application, one shared outcome, no "respective"/"each"/"both" language — one entry naming
both applicants is correct; the split applies only where the text itself marks the actions
as separate.

================================================================================
3. WHICH EVENTS TO RECORD
================================================================================

If it moved the case, created or removed a right, started or stopped a clock, or
authorised or ended a deprivation of liberty, record it — including non-judicial steps:
administrative decisions, prosecutorial rulings, enforcement actions. Include instances
that went nowhere: a request refused, an appeal declared inadmissible, a claim left
unexamined. A proceeding still running is still an event — a claim lodged and never
determined has a start_date_span, no decision_date_span, and pending_span filled instead of
an outcome.

RECORD EVERY INSTANCE THAT QUALIFIES, especially in a long run of similar-looking ones. The
asymmetry is what matters: an entry you wrote and should not have is visible and can be
removed later; an entry you never wrote is invisible and nothing downstream can recover it.

A DECISION DOES NOT NEED A DECIDING VERB. A body decides something just as clearly when
the sentence reports what it FOUND, CONCLUDED or DETERMINED as when it "dismissed" or
"granted" — read for whether something was settled, not for a specific verb.

WHAT NOT TO RECORD — trial management. Not everything asked and answered during a hearing
is its own entry. Record a ruling made during an already-open trial only if it authorised
or ended a deprivation of liberty, created or removed a right independent of the trial's
own merits (refusing chosen defence counsel, disqualifying the judge, refusing legal aid),
or disposed of the case or a charge in it. An ordinary ruling on the conduct of a hearing —
admitting evidence, refusing to sever counts, ruling there is a case to answer — is trial
management, not an event, even where it was refused: losing does not turn it into one.

Some things sit genuinely on the boundary — an inspection, an arrest, a registration made
at someone's request. Do not agonise and do not invent a rule: RECORD IT, and if the call
is genuinely hard, say so briefly in a note field if the schema allows one; otherwise just
record it and move on. Reserve doubt for cases where a careful colleague could disagree
with you, not for anything you merely had to think about.

Annotate what the judgment states, not what must have happened. An event you infer from
the shape of the procedure is not in the document and is not annotated.

================================================================================
4. THE CHAIN — follows
================================================================================

Every entry except the first of a track continues, reviews, appeals or enforces an earlier
one. Set follows and copy the linking words into follows_span. "Immediately before" is
PROCEDURAL, not chronological — two events merely consecutive in time with no procedural
relation are not linked.

Three continuations are missed constantly, beyond the obvious appeal:

  a. THE SAME BODY ACTING AGAIN IN THE SAME MATTER — a succession of decisions carrying one
     process forward (seizes an asset, values it, resolves to sell it, offers it, awards
     it, transfers ownership) is one entry per step, each following the one before.
  b. A NEW PHASE CONTINUING AN EARLIER ONE — a trial follows the investigation that
     produced the charges; enforcement follows the judgment it enforces; a judicial
     challenge follows the administrative decision it contests. The bodies differing is not
     a reason to break the chain.
  c. A REMITTAL GOING BACK DOWN — the fresh hearing follows the quashing, not the original
     first-instance decision.

THE TEST IS "THE SAME MATTER CONTINUED", NOT "THE NEXT THING THAT HAPPENED". Three things
are NOT continuations, each starting its own track: proceedings against a different person
(a co-accused tried separately is their own track); a new matter arising later (a fresh
investigation into new allegations does not continue a closed case); a parallel track
(civil and criminal proceedings over the same facts, neither continuing the other). When
unsure, leave them unlinked — a missing link is a visible gap, a wrong one is a false
history that reads as authoritative.

LINKING IS NOT MERGING. When one entry continues another, keep BOTH entries and link them —
do not fold a filing's date into the decision that answered it, or vice versa.

Before finishing, check every entry whose follows is empty and ask what came before it in
this matter. A genuinely separate track or the earliest thing recorded are legitimate
answers — an entry that plainly continues something already recorded and is left unlinked
is the error that turns a procedural history back into an unordered list.

================================================================================
5. THE FIELDS AND THEIR VOCABULARIES
================================================================================

Two fields are judgements you make from the event's own content, filled on EVERY event
regardless of whether the document uses the exact word:

instance_level — which rung:
  a first hearing of a claim, charge or application         -> first instance
  a review of a lower body's decision on the merits          -> appeal
  a review limited to points of law                          -> cassation
  a step inside a criminal investigation (warrant, arrest,
  a prosecutor's refusal, an expert order)                   -> investigative
  an internal review by an administrative body                -> administrative review
  a review of an already-decided stage by a body outside the
  ordinary appeal ladder, after finality                      -> case review
  a case reopened after a final decision                       -> reopening

outcome — which result, filled only where something was ASKED and ANSWERED (an arrest, a
seizure, a warrant are things DONE, not requests decided — leave outcome out; instance_level
is still filled):
  the body ruled on the substance, whichever way               -> merits decided
  a claim or appeal refused without reaching merits              -> inadmissible
  sent back to a lower body                                       -> remitted
  withdrawn, discontinued or settled                                -> settled or withdrawn
  a real outcome none of these fit                                    -> other
There is no value for "granted a request" as distinct from "ruled on the merits": a body
that grants a request (appointing a guardian, ordering a release) has ruled on the
substance of it, so it is merits decided. A housekeeping order deciding nothing (a transfer
on jurisdictional grounds) is other, not merits decided. `other` is a last resort: a claim
left unexamined is inadmissible, not other.

Two more judgements, same treatment, evidenced by the event's own content:

proceeding_type — the branch of law, on every event:
  a criminal charge, conviction, sentence, arrest, detention, prosecution   -> criminal
  a civil claim between private parties, damages, contract, family, property -> civil
  a decision by a state body outside a court — a licence, a benefit, a
  disciplinary or vetting body, a public-law claim against the state         -> administrative
  a challenge to a law's or measure's constitutionality                      -> constitutional
  a professional or judicial disciplinary proceeding                        -> disciplinary
  a real branch none of these fit                                            -> other
  not enough said to tell                                                    -> unknown

outcome_direction — did THIS decision come out for or against the future Strasbourg
applicant, filled under the same condition as outcome (an act with no outcome has no
direction either):
  the claim granted, the charge not made out, the appeal that sought this
  result succeeded, in full                                     -> favourable to applicant
  the claim dismissed, the charge made out, the appeal that
  sought this result failed, in full                            -> unfavourable to applicant
  THIS decision itself grants something AND denies something on
  the SAME claim (a conviction with a reduced sentence, damages
  awarded below what was sought where framed as a partial award) -> mixed
  remitted, referred onward, left unexamined, inadmissible —
  nothing granted or denied on the merits, nothing to be
  favourable, unfavourable or mixed ABOUT                        -> unknown
Test: reading only THIS decision, would the applicant say they partly won and partly lost
on the SAME claim? If yes, mixed. A later proceeding reversing an earlier one does not make
either event mixed — each keeps its own single direction, the case's ups and downs live in
the follows chain. A procedural side-effect (costs, legal aid) that is not the substance of
the claim does not make an otherwise-lost claim mixed. Direction tracks the claim or charge
actually being decided, not every incidental ruling inside the decision.

matter — what the event was substantively ABOUT (bail, damages, arrest), a DIFFERENT AXIS
from proceeding_type (the track). NOT filled on every event — an appeal, a cassation
review, a remittal are usually not substantively about any of these; leave it out entirely
unless the event's own content is recognisably one of:
  Arrest        the initial act of apprehending or taking into custody
  Detention     placing, continuing, extending, reviewing or ending pre-trial/remand
                detention — the deprivation of liberty itself; contrast Bail
  Bail          application for, grant, refusal or review of release pending trial
  Charge        the formal act of charging or indicting
  Conviction    a trial verdict or appeal against one — conviction, acquittal, sentence
  Investigation an investigative step short of a charge or detention decision — a search, a
                seizure, an expert order, a request refused during an investigation
  Damages       a claim for damages or compensation, and any decision on it
  Custody       custody of or guardianship over a person — parental custody, a
                child-abduction return, legal capacity/guardianship of an adult. NOT
                detention.
  Employment    a labour dispute — dismissal, reinstatement, wages, terms of employment
  Pension       a pension, social-security or welfare-benefit entitlement
  Property      a dispute over property, restitution, tenancy, or a planning/permit
                decision
  Extradition   an extradition, deportation or removal decision, or its execution
  LegalAid      application for, grant or refusal of legal aid or legal-aid counsel
  Vetting       granting, refusing or revoking a security clearance or vetting status
  Disciplinary  a professional or employment disciplinary proceeding — suspending,
                revoking or restoring a licence to practise
  Other         the event IS about one identifiable matter the list does not name
  Unknown       clearly about one of these matters, but the text does not say which
Give more than one value where one event genuinely rules on more than one matter (an order
continuing detention while setting bail conditions is Detention + Bail). Neither Other nor
Unknown is a default for an event with no substantive matter at all — that gets no matter
field at all, the same way an act with no result gets no outcome.

authority_kind — the deciding body:
  Judicial        a formal court or tribunal within the national judiciary
  Prosecutorial   a public prosecutor's office or police/investigative body
  Administrative  an executive, municipal, regulatory or welfare authority
  Disciplinary    a professional regulatory or internal disciplinary body

party side (in each party's role_span, in your own words from what the text shows):
  Initiating   the party who moved the authority to act at THIS instance — claimant,
               plaintiff, prosecution, appellant, the party seeking review or reopening
  Responding   the party the proceeding was brought against at THIS instance — defendant,
               accused, respondent on appeal
  ThirdParty   joined without being on either side — intervener, a body appearing to be
               heard
The deciding body of THIS event is NEVER a party to it, though it may be a party to a
DIFFERENT event in the chain (a prosecutor who decided at first instance may be the
responding party on appeal). Sides usually swap on review — whoever lost below is usually
the appellant above; read who brought THIS instance from THIS entry's own words, never by
copying the role across from the entry it follows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PARTIES — every entry needs them
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Somebody brought every step and somebody answered it. Name the party itself, never their
lawyer — a lawyer acting for a party is representation, not participation, and is recorded
only where the lawyer was themselves a party.

role_span must show STANDING IN THIS INSTANCE — who brought it and who answered it — not
the most recent thing they did inside it. A party who files a motion partway through does
NOT thereby become the party who brought the instance: if the applicant sued and the
respondent later moved to strike out, the applicant still brought it.

THE OTHER SIDE IS OFTEN NAMED ONLY ONCE. A first-instance judgment names both sides; the
appeal names only whoever appealed, because the other side is already known to the reader.
Where the entry this one follows recorded an opposing party and nothing suggests they
dropped out, record that party here too — otherwise they vanish from the upper half of the
chain.

ONE PARTY IS A CORRECT ANSWER. NO PARTIES IS A CORRECT ANSWER. Many entries genuinely have
one — an application to an authority, a prosecutor acting of his own motion, a review
opened ex officio. Do not manufacture a second party, and never reach for the deciding body
OF THIS ENTRY to fill the gap: the body in this entry's authority_span is not available as
this entry's missing party, precisely because it is the nearest name to hand.

BUT AN AUTHORITY IS THE PARTY WHEN ITS OWN DECISION IS THE THING UNDER REVIEW — the
single most-missed party in the corpus. A permit is refused by a District Authority; the
applicant appeals to a Regional Government, which refuses too; he appeals to an
Administrative Court, which quashes the Regional Government's decision. On the first two
entries those bodies decide. On the third, the court decides and the REGIONAL GOVERNMENT IS
THE RESPONDING PARTY. The test is who is DECIDING THIS ENTRY: everyone else named is
eligible, including a public body, including one that decided an earlier entry in the
chain.

THE FILER OR MOVANT IS A PARTY, side Initiating, whoever it is — including a prosecutor
bringing a case of their own motion or an administrative body applying for a measure. Do
not let the deciding body of THIS event crowd out who moved it.

An empty parties list says the text names no party, and is worth writing — it records that
you looked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELAY, POSTPONEMENT, FINALITY AND PENDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where the text says a case lay dormant or was stayed between two dates, record it in
inactivity with the bounding spans and any words attributing the cause. Where a hearing was
adjourned and later resumed, record it in adjournments. Length-of-proceedings is a core
Article 6 complaint and the delays are usually explicit.

pending_span: copy in words showing a matter was still outstanding when the judgment was
written — "the appeal is currently pending", "has yet to rule". A proceeding recorded this
way has a start_date_span, no decision_date_span, and no outcome — exactly like a filing
half of a §2.1 split. What tells the two apart is only that a pending proceeding's decision
was never written, where a filing's decision exists elsewhere in your own list of entries.

non_occurring_span: fill where the text narrates something that was NOT done — "the
applicant did not appeal", "no request for release was ever lodged" — rather than something
that happened. Type and classify the event as what it would have been had it happened,
quote the words stating the negation, and give it no decision_date_span — nothing was
decided, because nothing happened. Distinct from pending: a pending proceeding is real and
under way, just not yet decided; a non-occurring one never started.

finality_span: copy in words showing a decision was final or that remedies were exhausted.

================================================================================
6. WHEN TO WRITE "unknown" AND WHEN TO OMIT
================================================================================

OMIT the field entirely when the text gives you nothing and there is no escape value —
dates, quotes, names.

WRITE the escape value (unknown) when the field has a closed vocabulary, the event exists,
and the text does not tell you which value applies. Unknown is a real answer meaning "the
document does not say" — it is not the same as omitting the field.

NEVER GUESS from background knowledge of the legal system. If the judgment does not say
the Regional Court sat on appeal, instance_level is unknown even where you know that
country's regional courts usually do. You are annotating the document, not the
jurisdiction.

================================================================================
7. IDENTITY — WHEN ARE TWO MENTIONS THE SAME EVENT
================================================================================

Two mentions are the SAME event when they share the same deciding authority and the same
decision date. Naming variants are the same authority ("the Regional Court", "the Sofia
Regional Court", "the Sofia City Regional Court" are one body where the text is plainly
referring to the same one). Same authority, different dates -> different events: the
filing and its decision are always two events once split under §2.1, this carries no
exception. A date given only as a year matches a full date in that year where the
authority and surrounding narrative agree.

Filing events have no decision to match on — match them on authority and start_date
instead. A filing event never matches a decision event, even for the same authority and
proceeding: they are the two halves of a split pair and must be kept apart.

================================================================================
8. BEFORE YOU FINISH — COVERAGE CHECK
================================================================================

List every body that decided anything about these applicants — wider than courts: courts,
tribunals, prosecutors at every level, municipal councils, ministries, mayors, governors,
social assistance and welfare departments (granting, refusing, increasing or reducing an
allowance is a dated event with an acting body), police and enforcement officers (a search,
an arrest, a seizure, a payment order carried out), the end of a detention ("the applicant
was released" is an event with a date even where no authority is named — record it with
authority_kind_span omitted rather than dropping it), medical and social assessment bodies
whose findings decided something, care homes, hospitals, guardianship authorities,
registrars. EVERY one of them must be the authority_span of at least one entry. The
non-judicial periphery is what gets missed: a welfare decision or a police action reads as
background narrative rather than as a step it actually is.

HEDGED LANGUAGE IS NOT A REASON TO DROP AN ENTRY. "It appears that the order was lifted in
May 1996" is the document's own uncertainty about a fact it still asserts happened — copy
"it appears that" into the span along with the rest.

Where the document names a person at all — applicant, lawyer, guardian, relative, witness,
co-accused — give them an entry in persons, and copy a gender cue (an honorific, pronoun or
gendered noun) if the document marks them anywhere, even paragraphs after they are first
named.

Be exhaustive about evidence and silent about judgement. If the document does not say, do
not supply it.
