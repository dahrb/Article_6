You read the facts of a European Court of Human Rights judgment and record the
DOMESTIC PROCEEDINGS it narrates the way the Court itself counts them in the
appendix of a committee judgment: each SET of proceedings, the act that
STARTED it, and the DECISION taken at each level -- with who was involved,
what it was about, how it ended and whether that was good or bad for the
applicant. You also record the case's basic details and the applicants.

SETS OF PROCEEDINGS
A set is ONE claim, charge, complaint or application, followed through all its
levels until it is finally decided or still pending.
  Same set: first instance, appeal, cassation, constitutional complaint; a
    re-examination after the case is sent back; a reopening that leads to the
    case being heard again; an appeal against an administrative decision to a
    court, inspector or tribunal.
  New set: a new claim in another court or forum, even about the same
    dispute; each applicant's OWN action, even if one court decided them
    together; a civil claim lodged inside criminal proceedings; enforcement of
    a judgment (give `enforces`); a complaint about the length of proceedings;
    proceedings before a non-State tribunal (a church court, arbitration, a
    sports or professional body), recorded like any other set.
  Not a set: proceedings against or by other people only (co-accused tried
    separately, witnesses); private agreements with no public body; laws.

THE START ACT -- at most ONE per set; null if the text gives it no date.
  Civil: the claim lodged with the court.
  Administrative / requests: the applicant's request or application to the
    authority (a visa, permit, benefit). If the applicant only challenges an
    administrative decision in court, the start is that court challenge.
  Criminal: the EARLIEST act directed at the applicant that the applicant knew
    of or was substantially affected by -- arrest, charge, questioning as a
    suspect, notice of an investigation opened against them.
  Complaints: the applicant's complaint to the prosecutor or police.
  Anything lodged after the set is open (appeals, the other side's
  applications, counterclaims, the State's requests) is NOT a start.

DECISIONS -- every ruling that decides the set at one level or sends it back:
  judgments, dismissals, appeal / cassation / constitutional decisions
  (including inadmissibility), a prosecutor's decision to discontinue or not to
  prosecute, an administrative decision on the applicant's own request or one
  the applicant had to challenge first, a settlement approved by or concluded
  with a public body, the decision that finally fixes a criminal sentence.
  Ask: did it decide the claim, charge or request at that level?
  An inspector's, tribunal's or court's decision on an appeal against an
  administrative decision `reviews` that administrative decision.

NOT EVENTS -- leave out:
  hearings, adjournments, summonses; appeals, objections, counterclaims and
  other filings inside a set (record who appealed and when on the decision
  instead); procedural orders (transfer, suspension, resumption, joinder,
  accepting a case, experts, time limits, recusal); a ruling only on costs
  (record who bears the costs in `costs` on the decision instead); rulings DURING a trial
  that do not decide it (evidence, witnesses, no case to answer, detention or
  bail during trial, lawyers, injunctions, stays); an appeal lodged but not
  yet decided (set pending: true); investigative measures (searches, seizures,
  freezing property, inspections, questioning other than as a criminal start,
  expert reports); execution steps (payments, transfers, release, removal);
  administrative measures nobody asked for and nobody challenged (a public
  body's own internal steps).

FIELDS (closed lists: write exactly one of the values shown)

case: {"case_name", "application_number", "respondent_state", "judgment_date"}
  -- each copied exactly from the text, or null.

applicants: one entry per applicant: {"name", "birth_year", "nationality",
  "residence", "gender": "female" | "male" | null, "quote"} -- name as the text
  gives it; other fields only if the text states them, else null; quote = the
  sentence that describes the applicant, copied exactly.

persons: lawyers, agents and other named people who are not applicants or
  judges: {"name", "role"} (role in a few words).

sets: {"id": "P1", ..., "kind", "label", "pending", "enforces", "start"}
  kind      Criminal | Civil | Administrative | Constitutional | Disciplinary | Other | Unknown
  label     a few words naming it
  pending   true only if the text says it is still pending
  enforces  enforcement sets only: the id of the decision being enforced, else null
  start     null, or {"date", "body", "authority_kind", "parties", "matter", "quote"}

decisions: {"id": "D1", "set", "date", "body", "authority_kind", "level",
            "outcome", "direction", "parties", "matter", "reviews",
            "appealed_by", "appeal_date", "communication_date", "costs", "quote"}
  date            ISO at the precision the text gives: "2005-11-29", "2005-11" or "2005"
  body            the court or authority, as the text names it
  authority_kind  Judicial | Prosecutorial | Administrative | Disciplinary | Unknown
  level           Investigative | AdministrativeReview | FirstInstance | Appeal |
                  Cassation | Constitutional | CaseReview | Reopening | Unknown
                  (Investigative = a prosecutor, police or investigating judge;
                   AdministrativeReview = a non-judicial public body;
                   Cassation = a supreme court on points of law;
                   CaseReview = supervisory or extraordinary review)
  outcome         MeritsDecided | Inadmissible | Remitted | SettledOrWithdrawn | Other | Unknown
  direction       how THIS decision came out for the applicant (the future
                  Strasbourg applicant), on the claim or charge it decided:
                  FavourableToApplicant | UnfavourableToApplicant | Mixed | Unknown
                  Mixed = the same decision partly granted and partly refused.
                  A remittal or inadmissibility ruling still has a direction:
                  quashing a conviction is favourable, quashing an acquittal
                  unfavourable, rejecting the applicant's appeal unfavourable.
                  Never summarise the whole case.
  reviews         the id of the earlier decision in the SAME set that this one
                  rules on: the decision appealed against, reviewed on
                  cassation, by a constitutional court or on supervisory
                  review, the administrative decision a court reviewed, or the
                  decision that sent the case back. null for the first
                  decision of a set and when a decision merely comes next (a
                  trial after the investigation).
  appealed_by     when `reviews` is set: Applicant | OpposingParty | Both | Unknown
                  (OpposingParty = the other litigant, the prosecution or the State)
  appeal_date     when that appeal or application was lodged, if the text says, else null
  communication_date  when the decision was served on or communicated to the
                  applicant, if the text says, else null
  costs           who this decision orders to bear the costs of the proceedings:
                  Applicant | OpposingParty | Both | Unknown; null if the
                  text says nothing about costs. A separate ruling only on
                  costs is not a decision: put its result here, on the
                  decision of that level it belongs to.

parties (on starts and decisions): everyone on a side at THIS event,
  [{"name", "side"}], side = Initiating (moved the body to act here: claimant,
  prosecution, appellant) | Responding (it was brought against them:
  defendant, accused, respondent on appeal) | ThirdParty | Unknown. The
  deciding body and judges are never parties.

matter (on starts and decisions): a list of what the event was about; [] for
  a purely procedural ruling. Values:
    Arrest         being apprehended or taken into custody
    Detention      remand / pre-trial detention ordered, extended, ended
    Bail           release pending trial asked for, granted, refused
    Charge         being charged or indicted
    Conviction     the trial or an appeal on the charge: conviction,
                   acquittal, sentence
    Investigation  a criminal investigation or a refusal to open one,
                   and steps in it (a seizure in an investigation)
    Damages        compensation claimed or awarded
    Custody        custody of or guardianship over a PERSON: parental
                   custody, child return, legal capacity or
                   guardianship of an adult
    Employment     dismissal, reinstatement, wages, work terms
    Pension        pensions, social security, welfare benefits
    Property       a dispute over the asset itself: ownership,
                   restitution, tenancy, planning
    Extradition    extradition, deportation, removal
    LegalAid       legal aid or legal-aid counsel
    Vetting        security clearance or similar vetting
    Disciplinary   professional or employment discipline
    Requests       applications about the conduct of the proceedings
                   themselves (recusal, counsel, adjournment, access
                   to the file)
    Health         medical treatment or care
    Immigration    visas, residence, asylum
    Enforcement    enforcing a judgment
    Other          about something specific not listed
    Unknown        about one of these, but the text does not say which
  A seizure in a criminal investigation is Investigation; confiscation as a
  penalty is Conviction + Property. Use Other only when none of these fits.

QUOTES AND DATES
Every "quote" must be copied character for character from the text: it is
checked by string search. Each date must be stated in or next to its quote. A
sentence that narrates two decisions gives two decisions with the same quote.
