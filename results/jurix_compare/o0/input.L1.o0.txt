Based on the document provided, the domestic proceedings in Bulgaria are as follows:

**1. Application for Declaration of Total Legal Incapacity (2000–2001)**
*   **The Proceeding:** At the request of the applicant's relatives, the Ruse regional prosecutor applied to the Ruse Regional Court to have the applicant declared totally legally incapacitated.
*   **The Outcome:** On 20 November 2000, the court declared the applicant **partially incapacitated** (rather than totally), finding that while his ability to manage his affairs was impaired due to schizophrenia, his condition was not serious enough to warrant total incapacity. This judgment was upheld by the Veliko Tarnovo Court of Appeal on 12 April 2001.

**2. Requests for Restoration of Legal Capacity (2004–2005)**
*   **The Proceeding:** The applicant, via his lawyer, asked the public prosecutor’s office to apply to the Regional Court to restore his legal capacity.
*   **The Outcome:** The regional prosecutor **refused to bring the action** on 10 August 2005, citing medical opinions and the view of the social care home that the applicant was unable to cope on his own. This refusal was upheld by the appellate prosecutor (11 October 2005) and the Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office at the Supreme Court of Cassation (29 November 2005).

**3. Application for Judicial Review of the Mayor’s Decision (2005–2006)**
*   **The Proceeding:** After the Mayor of Rila refused the applicant's request to bring a court action for his release from partial guardianship, the applicant’s lawyer applied to the Dupnitsa District Court for a judicial review of that refusal.
*   **The Outcome:** On 10 March 2006, the court **dismissed the application**. While the court accepted the application as admissible (despite a dispute over whether the guardian had signed the lawyer's authority form), it ruled on the merits that the guardian had no legitimate interest in contesting the mayor's refusal because the guardian could apply for the applicant's release independently.
