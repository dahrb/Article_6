Based on the document provided, the domestic proceedings in Belgium are as follows:

### 1. Administrative Visa Applications
*   **Initial Application:** The applicants applied for "humanitarian visas" (limited territorial validity) to enter Belgium and seek asylum. The **Aliens Office** refused these applications on September 13, 2016, arguing that such visas are for short-term stays and not for those intending to settle permanently or seek asylum.
*   **Second Application:** Following the Aliens Office's suggestion, the applicants applied for a different type of visa (long-stay), which was also rejected in December 2016.

### 2. Proceedings before the Aliens Appeals Board (Judicial Appeal Body)
*   **First Stay of Execution (Oct 7, 2016):** The Board ordered a stay of the initial visa refusals and told the State to make new decisions considering the danger in Syria.
*   **Second Refusal and Stay (Oct 14, 2016):** The Aliens Office issued new refusals on October 10, 2016, using the same reasons. The Board again ordered a stay of execution on October 14, citing the serious risk of a violation of Article 3 of the Convention.
*   **Third Refusal and Direct Order (Oct 20, 2016):** After another refusal by the Aliens Office on October 17, the Board ordered a stay of execution and, due to the imminent danger and the State's non-compliance, directed the State to issue visas or *laissez-passers* within 48 hours.
*   **Dismissal of Set-Aside Applications (March 24, 2017):** The Board dismissed the applicants' requests to have the visa refusals set aside, ruling that the original September 13 decisions had become final, meaning the applicants no longer had standing to challenge subsequent decisions.

### 3. Proceedings before the Conseil d’État (Administrative Court of Law)
*   **First Appeal (Feb 8, 2018):** The court dismissed the applicants' appeal against the Board's March 6, 2017, decision to lift the stay of execution.
*   **Second Appeal (May 17, 2018):** The court dismissed appeals against the Board's March 24, 2017, judgments. It based this on a CJEU ruling that the Visa Code only applies to stays under 90 days and not to asylum requests.

### 4. Civil Proceedings for Enforcement (Brussels Courts)
*   **TPI (Court of First Instance):**
    *   On October 25, 2016, the acting president ordered the State to comply with the Board's October 20 judgment under penalty of a daily fine.
    *   On November 7, 2016, another judge overturned this, ruling the application unfounded because the State intended to appeal the legality of the judgment.
*   **Brussels Court of Appeal (Dec 7, 2016):** The court varied the TPI's order, ruling that the State's failure to comply was an "illegal action." It ordered the State to issue the visas/laissez-passers and imposed a penalty of 1,000 EUR per day per applicant.
*   **Enforcement Judge Disputes (Dec 2016 – Dec 2017):**
    *   The State sought a stay of execution of the December 7 judgment.
    *   The applicants sought to enforce the penalty payments.
    *   On June 30, 2017, a Dutch-language division of the Court of Appeal ruled that the Board's October 20 judgment (and consequently the December 7 judgment) was no longer operative because the original visa refusals had become final.
    *   On December 20, 2017, the French-language enforcement judge declared the applicants' action devoid of purpose following the June 30 ruling.
