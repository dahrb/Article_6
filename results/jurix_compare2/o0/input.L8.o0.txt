Based on the document provided, the domestic legal proceedings in Russia involving Andrey Anatolyevich Klimentyev are as follows:

### 1. First Trial (Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court)
*   **Charges:** Embezzlement and other economic crimes (charged March 7, 1995).
*   **Outcome (April 21, 1997):** The court found the applicant guilty of some charges and not guilty of most. He was sentenced to one and a half years in prison (time already served in pre-trial detention) and some of his property was confiscated.

### 2. First Appeal (Supreme Court)
*   **Outcome (July 1997):** The Supreme Court quashed the conviction and remitted the case back to the Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court for a new trial.

### 3. Second Trial (Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court)
*   **Detention Proceedings:**
    *   On March 30, 1998, the court initially rejected a prosecutor's motion to detain the applicant.
    *   On April 2, 1998, the court granted a renewed motion for detention based on obstruction of justice and breach of court orders. The applicant was arrested, and the court ordered the trial to be conducted *in camera* (privately).
*   **Interlocutory Motions:**
    *   The court rejected a request to dismiss the presiding judge for bias (May 19, 1998).
    *   The court rejected a request to admit Mrs. Moskalenko as a lawyer during the trial (May 19, 1998).
*   **Outcome (May 27, 1998):** The court found the applicant guilty of misappropriation, embezzlement, larceny, bribery, and attempt not to return money from abroad. He was sentenced to six years in prison and the confiscation of property. He was also ordered to pay damages to the "Oka" shipyard and three other companies.

### 4. Second Appeal/Cassation (Supreme Court)
*   **Challenge to Detention:** The Supreme Court rejected an appeal against the April 2 detention order (May 19, 1998), stating that national law did not allow separate appeals against such orders.
*   **Main Appeal Outcome (August 10, 1998):** The Supreme Court confirmed the Regional Court's judgment with some alterations (dropping certain charges and reclassifying crimes based on the 1997 Criminal Code). It upheld the six-year prison sentence and rejected the defense's arguments regarding inadmissible evidence and inadequate representation.

### 5. Other Related Proceedings
*   **Property Dispute:** Following an investigation in 1995, the prosecutor's office froze the applicant's ship (*Stavanger*) and a drydock. The ship was eventually sold to the Makhachkala International Sea Port, and the proceeds were transferred by the prosecutor to the "Oka" shipyard before a final conviction was reached. The Regional Court later noted this was a violation of the Code of Criminal Procedure but deducted the funds from the damages the applicant owed the shipyard.
*   **Prior Conviction:** In February 1999, a previous conviction from 1982 was quashed and the file closed.
