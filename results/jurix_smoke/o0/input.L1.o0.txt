**1. Declaration of Partial Incapacity (Ruse Regional Court & Veliko Tarnovo Court of Appeal)**
*   **What happened:** In 2000, at the request of the applicant's relatives, the Ruse Regional Court declared the applicant partially incapacitated due to schizophrenia, finding that his ability to manage his own affairs was impaired. The applicant appealed this decision, but the Veliko Tarnovo Court of Appeal upheld the judgment on April 12, 2001.

**2. Request for Restoration of Legal Capacity (Prosecution Service)**
*   **What happened:** In 2004, the applicant’s lawyer asked the public prosecutor’s office to apply to the Regional Court to restore his legal capacity. After a medical assessment in 2005, the regional prosecutor refused to bring the action, arguing that the applicant was unable to cope on his own and the social care home was the most suitable place for him. This refusal was upheld by the appellate prosecutor and the Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office at the Supreme Court of Cassation in November 2005.

**3. Judicial Review of the Mayor’s Refusal (Dupnitsa District Court)**
*   **What happened:** After the Mayor of Rila refused to bring a court action for the applicant's release from partial guardianship, the applicant’s lawyer applied for judicial review. The court first dealt with a procedural dispute regarding whether the lawyer had valid authority (since the guardian had not signed the representation form). The court eventually ruled the application admissible because the guardian endorsed it during a hearing. However, on March 10, 2006, the court dismissed the application on its merits, ruling that the guardian could have applied for the release independently.
