Based on the document provided, the domestic proceedings in Russia are as follows:

### 1. First Trial (Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court)
*   **Charges:** Embezzlement and other economic crimes.
*   **Outcome (April 21, 1997):** The court found the applicant guilty on some charges and not guilty on most. He was sentenced to one and a half years in prison (time already served in pre-trial detention) and some of his property was confiscated.

### 2. First Appeal (Supreme Court)
*   **Outcome (July 1997):** The Supreme Court quashed the conviction and remitted the case back to the Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court for a new trial.

### 3. Second Trial (Nizhniy Novgorod Regional Court)
*   **Procedural Events:**
    *   **Detention:** After an initial rejection, the court granted a motion to detain the applicant on April 2, 1998, citing obstruction of justice and breach of court orders. The court also ordered the trial to be conducted *in camera* (privately).
    *   **Defense Motions:** The court rejected requests to dismiss the presiding judge for bias and refused to admit Mrs. Moskalenko as a lawyer during the trial.
    *   **Evidence:** The court admitted witness statements from Norway and Germany without the witnesses being present, as well as documents in English translated during the trial.
*   **Outcome (May 27, 1998):** The applicant was found guilty of misappropriation, embezzlement, larceny, bribery, and attempt not to return money from abroad. He was sentenced to six years in prison and ordered to pay damages to the "Oka" shipyard and three other companies.
*   **Additional Orders:** The court issued "private orders" noting that investigators had violated procedural rules (e.g., not informing the defendant about expert reports and prematurely transferring the applicant's frozen funds to the "Oka" shipyard).

### 4. Second Appeal/Cassation (Supreme Court)
*   **Arguments:** The defense challenged the admissibility of evidence, the impartiality of the court, the timing of civil claims, and the adequacy of legal representation.
*   **Outcome (August 10, 1998):** The Supreme Court confirmed the judgment of the Regional Court with some alterations. It dropped certain charges and reclassified some crimes based on the 1997 Criminal Code, but upheld the six-year prison sentence. It ruled that there were no significant breaches of procedural law.

### 5. Other Legal Matters
*   **Previous Conviction:** In February 1999, a conviction from 1982 was quashed and the file closed.
*   **Property Dispute:** The Nizhniy Novgorod prosecutor's office froze the applicant's ship (*Stavanger*) and a drydock in 1995. The proceeds from the sale of the ship were transferred to the "Oka" shipyard in 1996 before the trial concluded; the Regional Court later noted this was a procedural violation but deducted those funds from the final damages awarded to the shipyard.
