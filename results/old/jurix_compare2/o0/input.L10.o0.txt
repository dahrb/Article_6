Based on the document provided, the domestic legal proceedings in Belgium are as follows:

### 1. Visa Applications and Administrative Refusals
*   **Initial Application (August 2016):** The applicants applied for "humanitarian visas" (limited territorial validity) at the Belgian embassy in Beirut to travel to Belgium and seek asylum.
*   **First Refusal (September 2016):** The Aliens Office refused the visas, arguing that such visas are for short-term stays and not for those intending to settle permanently or apply for asylum.
*   **Alternative Application (December 2016):** Following the Aliens Office's suggestion, the applicants applied for a different type of visa (long-stay), which was also rejected.

### 2. Proceedings before the Aliens Appeals Board (AAB)
*   **First Stay of Execution (October 2016):** Under an "extremely urgent procedure," the AAB ordered a stay of the September 13 refusal and instructed the State to make a new decision based on the dangerous situation in Syria.
*   **Second Refusal and Stay (October 2016):** The Aliens Office issued new refusals on October 10 with the same reasoning. On October 14, the AAB again ordered a stay of execution and demanded a new decision with adequate legal grounds.
*   **Third Refusal and Order to Issue (October 2016):** The Aliens Office refused again on October 17. On October 20, the AAB ordered a stay and, due to the imminent danger and the State's non-compliance, directed the State to issue the applicants visas or *laissez-passers* within 48 hours.
*   **Final Dismissal (March 2017):** The applicants sought to have the refusal decisions set aside. On March 24, the AAB dismissed the application and lifted the stays of execution, ruling that the original September 13 decisions had become final and the applicants no longer had standing to challenge subsequent decisions.

### 3. Proceedings before the Conseil d’État (Council of State)
*   **Appeal on Points of Law (February 2018):** The Conseil d’État dismissed the applicants' appeal against the AAB's March 6 decision to lift the stay of execution.
*   **Appeal on Points of Law (May 2018):** The Conseil d’État dismissed the applicants' appeal against the AAB's March 24 decision. It based this on a CJEU ruling stating that the Visa Code does not apply to asylum seekers seeking stays longer than 90 days.

### 4. Enforcement Proceedings (Civil Courts)
*   **First Instance (October/November 2016):** The applicants sought urgent enforcement of the AAB's October 20 order. The President of the French-language Court of First Instance (TPI) initially ordered the State to comply under penalty of a daily fine. However, upon an *inter partes* action, another judge declared the application unfounded, ruling that the State could challenge the judgment via appeal.
*   **Brussels Court of Appeal (December 2016):** The Court varied the TPI's order, ruling that the State's failure to comply was an "illegal action." It ordered the State to issue the visas/laissez-passers and imposed a penalty of 1,000 EUR per day per applicant.
*   **Conflict of Enforcement (December 2016 – June 2017):**
    *   The Belgian State sought a stay of execution through the Dutch-speaking TPI and subsequently the Court of Appeal.
    *   The applicants sought execution of the penalty payments through the French-speaking TPI.
    *   **Final Outcome (June 2017):** A Dutch-language division of the Court of Appeal ruled that the AAB's October 20 judgment (and consequently the Court of Appeal's December 7 judgment) was no longer operative because the original September 13 visa refusals had become final.
*   **Final Closure (December 2017):** The French-language enforcement judge declared the applicants' action devoid of purpose following the June 30 Court of Appeal ruling.
